Move 3 · the sheet follows the thumb · the More sheet over the home · page 2

Opened from the dock with a thumb. Closed by the same thumb, without reaching.

The More sheet rises over 200ms and dismisses on a tap anywhere on the scrim, which on a phone means the top of the screen. The dock test forbids that reach. The pull-to-refresh and the buddy drag already have gesture physics with a written contract (unified pointer and touch events, resistance, a cancel curve, no fight with the scroll). This is the third gesture, under the same contract, and it makes the sheet a thing the thumb is holding.

Frame 2 · held, 150px down

The sheet is 150px below rest because the thumb is 150px below where it landed. The scrim has lightened in step (30% at rest, 0% when the sheet is gone). No timer runs. Frame 3 · let go short of the line

Released at 150px, short of the line and without speed, the sheet settles back over 200ms. On H-69 a it settles on the spring, overshooting rest by a few pixels as drawn here; declined, it returns on the arrival curve. Let go past the line, or with a downward flick anywhere, and it leaves from where it is on the exit curve, the scrim finishing with it.

The contract, shared with the two gestures that ship

Unified pointer and touch events and no gesture library; resistance past the edge; a cancel curve when released short; no interference with a scroll: a drag begins only on the handle or when the sheet’s own list is at its top and the finger moves down. The finger’s speed at release decides with the distance. One hook holds it (the pull-to-refresh’s, generalised), so the third gesture and the fourth share one set of numbers.

Where else

The embed lightbox on a shop’s own website, which is a sheet over someone else’s page and today closes on a corner button. The palette on a phone, if it is ever a sheet. Never the palette on a desktop, which the keyboard opened and the keyboard closes, and never a dialog that carries a form mid-fill: a drag on a half-typed booking is a drag that loses words, and that sheet keeps its button.

What renders otherwise

The scrim tap and Escape stay, so nothing a keyboard or a switch user does changes. A reduced-motion reader’s sheet still follows the finger (tracking is not animation) and, let go, leaves in one frame. A pointer that is a mouse gets the tap and the button, as today; the handle is drawn for both and does nothing extra under a mouse.
