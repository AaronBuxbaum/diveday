The physics · one ladder, three curves, one press · page 1

Written once, read by CSS and JS alike.

Principle 5 was written one fix at a time: the bar, the exit curve, the disclosure, the menu. Each rule is right and the tree still holds ten durations and every timer as a copied number. This sheet is the same rules stated as a material: what a thing takes when it arrives, leaves, unfolds, or is held; how long; on which curve; and the events that get each. Nothing on it exceeds 250ms except the two ceilings the principle already grants.

The ladder · three rungs and two ceilings

--motion-quick150

--motion-base200

The default, as today. A figure rolling, a row sliding, a body arriving, a toast, a sheet leaving, a panel opening from its control.

A group of controls unfolding and folding, per child, staggered 50ms (ships on the board’s row menu as 260 in and 280 out; one rung replaces both).

ceiling400

A staggered disclosure as a whole (principle 5). Not a rung; a limit.

ceiling600

A drawn moment (budget rule 2): the swell at 320, the boat at 520. Unchanged, and never on a safety surface.

The rungs are Tailwind theme tokens beside the three curves, so duration-base is a utility and var(--motion-base) a value. The JS half reads the same token once (motionMs("base")) and a timer names a rung, never a number. motion-tokens.test.ts extends to refuse a bare millisecond in a component.

The three curves, and what takes each

Arrive · --ease-out-soft

Fast off the mark, settling. A digit arriving, a row sliding into place, a body arriving, a sheet rising, a panel opening. The default, as today.

Leave · --ease-in-soft

Slow off the mark, then away. A digit leaving, a row going, a toast dismissing, a sheet leaving from where the thumb let it go. Named -out, held by the exit-curve check, as today.

Weight · --ease-spring H-69 a

Overshoots and settles; claims the thing has weight. Rationed like coral: the unfolding menu (ships), and, on the owner’s call, the two places a finger gave weight: a sheet settling back from a short drag, and a pressed control letting go. Nothing that merely appears.

The press · every tappable thing, at rest and under a finger

Down takes no easing and no time: the thing is at 97% and on the tideline in the frame the finger lands. Up takes --motion-quick on the spring (H-69 a; the arrival curve otherwise). Transform and background only. The button’s active:scale-[0.98] today eases down over 200ms, which a 90ms tap never sees; the row, the chip, the tab and the ledger line do nothing.

What never moves

A refusal. role="alert" text renders in place, in one frame, beside the control. Motion beside a refusal is banned (budget rule 8) and stays so.

Anything while a field has focus. Budget rule 2 stands: nothing drawn moves while a person is typing, and neither does a row or a figure beside the field.

A state the surface does not yet have. On the counter the roll follows the optimistic commit principle 1 grants it; on the roll call it would follow the server’s, and the head count never rolls at all (H-69 c). A figure’s first paint swaps: nothing changed in front of the reader.

Money on a report. A total that was loaded with the page did not change; it is not a roll. Counting up to a figure claims scale, which the fact-of-scale rule reserves for divers and boats.

Anything, for a reduced-motion reader, except a thing under their own finger. The kill-switch already zeroes every duration and delay; the sheet still follows the thumb, because tracking a finger is not animation.

What this sheet changes in principle 5, and what it keeps

Keeps the 150–250ms rule, transform and opacity only, the exit curve and its name, the disclosure body that arrives and never its height, the 400ms stagger ceiling, no motion between pages, no cross-fade.

Adds three rungs as tokens and one reader for JS; the press as a rule for every tappable thing; the finger as a clock that outranks every timer; the roll and the slide as the two answers to “what changed”; and the scroll as a clock for the title, and nothing else.

Asks the owner to widen the spring’s ration from one use to three (a), and whether structural motion crosses onto the roll call at all (c). If either is declined, the boards on page 2 render the arrival curve and the swap, and nothing else on this sheet changes.
