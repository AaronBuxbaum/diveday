Two scroll-driven animations (animation-timeline: scroll(), animation-range: 0 120px), opacity and translate only, inside @supports (animation-timeline: scroll()). The scroll is the clock, so there is no duration to put on the ladder and no listener to throttle. The title itself does not shrink into the bar: a 34px heading scaled to half is a blur on a phone, and two labels crossing is the honest version of one morphing.

Where else

Every staff page with a 40/700 title on a phone: Counter check-in, Divers, Orders, a diver’s record, a departure. The public storefront keeps its own header (Harbor’s, the shop’s name in the shop’s face) and does not fold; a diver’s pages are short.

What renders otherwise

A browser without scroll-driven animations, a reduced-motion reader, and every desktop: the bar as it ships today, with the shop’s name. The fold is progressive enhancement in the literal sense, one @supports block, and its absence is never a missing page name for a screen reader, whose landmark is the page’s own h1 either way.
