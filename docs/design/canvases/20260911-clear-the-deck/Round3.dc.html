Clear the deck, round 3 — Console, the instrument that moves

Round 2 offered five surfaces. The owner's read: the problem is Reef itself, the instrument was the direction they liked, and the answer lies farther from the cutesy-ness. So this page stops offering options and draws one direction deep: Console — DiveDay as the shop's instrument. Every surface is a gauge you read, not a page you browse: the one number it exists to show, the lines beneath it, a state in a slot, and nothing decorative at all. It is the third time the instrument has been drawn. The first two lost on movement and on warmth; this one answers movement with round 1's three words and field, and answers warmth by not trying — daylight instead of a dark desk.

"I think my problem is with the Reef itself. I like when we were looking at DiveDay as an instrument, possibly moving farther away from the cutesy-ness."

What this settles

The pole. Reef was drawn on 09-01 to be warm and alive; Console is drawn to be exact. Everything that made Reef cutesy leaves, and leaves for good rather than moving to a quieter corner:

the hand — every drawn mark: the site tiles, the greeting's bubbles, the boat, the swell, the turtle, the rooms in the empty states coral — in every use: the accent, the deep, the tint, the moments, the mark's smallest bubble on a staff surface the sand — the warm ground, the shell, the tideline, the rope; the four water washes by the clock the panel on a bed — the 28px radius and the shadow; the radius ladder the dial, the chip, the pill, the tint — every colour that carried a state without a word, and every wash behind text the greeting and the display moment — "Good morning, Dana" at 44px; the biggest thing on a Console page is a number the moments — the water closing over the work, the count that fills with water, the diver's day drawn; a figure rolling is the one motion left the postcard's drawing — the recap becomes a printed log card: figures, sites, times, in the same system

And what an instrument means, stated so a guard can hold it: figures lead; every number is tabular in a mono face; a state is a word in a fixed slot; the day is a countdown; structure is hairlines and one ink rule; controls are square; two signal inks, both always beside a word; three schemes — day, depth, boat — of the same seven tokens; nothing on a surface that is not a fact.

The boards on this page

Console — the sheet: the seven tokens in three schemes, the two faces and five sizes, the six parts, the controls, the chrome, the words allowed in the slot. The home by day: Thursday at 6:40 AM on the desk, the countdown and the ladder. The home at depth: the same page at 6:10 PM with the lights off, souls counted home. Three phones: the counter at 6:40, the roll call on Mantis II at 6:58 in the boat scheme, and the home in a pocket with the three-word dock. All four are drawn on round 1's Three words with Ask, which is still call (a); Console holds on any of the six.

The recommendation

Stated so it can be disagreed with: adopt Console as the surface, on Three words with Ask, with Ashore-and-aboard's boat half as the roll call's home. It is the direction the owner has now named twice, with the two reasons it lost before answered in the drawing rather than in prose. It deletes more of Reef than any of round 2's five, because it deletes the idea of decoration rather than the decoration. Its risk is the one an instrument always carries: a shop that wanted a brochure gets a console — and the storefront, in the shop's colour and face, is where that shop still gets its brochure.

With this page, (b) and (f) are answered by the drawing — the decoration leaves, Geist stays — and (g) is recommended as entirely: the hand and the coral leave the product, and the recap is redrawn as a log card. (a), (c), (d) and (h) stand as round 1 and round 2 put them.

What no round changes

The name and the mark — the mark's coral bubble is the one coral left in the product, on DiveDay's own pages only. Harbor on the diver's side.
