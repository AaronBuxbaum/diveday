How the roll call gets its glare

  • A word, not a scheme. GLARE sits in the bar of the roll call and the counter, as a bordered word in mono. A tap turns the app's boat-mode skin on for that phone; the word fills to say so; a tap turns it off. Nothing else in the app has the switch.
  • The theme never does it. Light renders light; dark renders the app's night; the boat-mode skin has its own night variant when the phone is dark and glare is on. Spray and sun are a hand's call, which is what the skin was built for.
  • The count is the same count. The face, the gauge, the cells and the tap are the same parts in either skin; only the nine tokens change under them.

What changed from round 3's phones

  • The readout became the face at 40px on a phone; each boat on the home got its gauge, so the pocket reads like the desk.
  • The roll call is light by default, and its 128px black-whatever-the-phone-says rule leaves; the boat scheme becomes the glare word.
  • The cells sit on sheets on the home and on the paper elsewhere: a boat is a sheet even in a pocket.

The dock test, measured

  • Cells 60px and 64px on the roll call; names 18 and 20px; targets 48px; ink 14:1 on sand and 15.8:1 in glare; red 6.1:1 on sand; amber 4.7:1 on sand, the tightest pair on the paper.
  • The two who cannot board carry a sentence; the one not aboard carries the minutes left. Never colour alone, anywhere.

What it needs

The counter and the roll call already exist and already commit before they render; they take the face, the gauge and the cell from the sheet and lose the coral bar, the tinted rows and the drawn marks. The glare word wraps the boat-mode class the manifest already carries. The three-word dock replaces StaffTabBar.

Where it stops

Glare is the only place the crew chooses a skin, and it is a word, not a setting page. A shop that wants the roll call black always is asking for round 3's rule, which the dial's first stop shows and this page declines.

Its wow

A captain reads 9 of 10 and the one name in red from the wheel on a phone that looks like the shop's paper, taps once, and watches the figure roll to 10. When the sun comes round, one more tap and the page is white and strong. Nothing else on the screen.
