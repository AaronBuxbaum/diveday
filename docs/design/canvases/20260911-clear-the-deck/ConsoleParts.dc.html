Five additions to the sheet

Round 3 made Console exact. This round makes it an instrument in the hand: the reading gets a display, every count becomes a gauge with one segment per seat, every surface says when its reading was taken and whether it can be trusted, an empty reading is dashes, and the digits hold their width. Nothing here is a new colour, a drawing or a moment; each is a thing an instrument has always had.

The display

The one reading a surface exists to show sits on dark glass, even by day — the way a dive computer's screen is dark in sunlight. The most important number gets the most contrast, and the rest of the page stays matte. One per surface, never two.

The seat gauge

A boat has seats, so its count is a row of seats: filled for here, red for cannot board, amber for still to come, empty for unsold. A glance reads the boat; the legend under it reads the same in words. It replaces the meter wherever the count is people on a boat.

The telemetry line

An instrument says when its reading was taken. Every surface ends in one line: live and when, or saved on this phone and since when, or stale and what to do about it — the offline and freshness states the boat already tracks, in the instrument's own words, never a pill.

Why it earns its place

Contrast follows importance. The reading is why the surface exists; it gets 16:1 on black while the sentences get a matte page. It is the same glass everywhere. Day, depth and boat schemes all show the display as #0b0f12 with a #263036 edge; at depth it reads as a panel by its edge alone. One per surface. A second display is a dashboard, which is the thing Console is not. No data is dashes. A surface with nothing to read shows —— and says why beneath it; never a drawing, never a room. Cost: one component; the readout of round 3 becomes its content.

Why it earns its place

A boat is seats. Twelve segments are twelve seats, so the gauge is the boat, not a percentage of it. Four states, four inks — ink, red, amber, empty — and the legend beneath says the same in words, so the gauge is never the only carrier. Grey is booked-not-here: a seat sold to someone who has not walked up yet, on a boat whose check-in has not opened. It replaces the meter wherever the count is people on a boat; the meter stays for counts that are not seats (waivers signed, orders confirmed). On the phone the segments are 16px tall and the legend is the count words the counter already shows.

Why it earns its place

An instrument shows when it read. Live and the time; saved on this phone and since when; stale and what to do. The safety wording stays human. "Refresh before you rely on it" is principle 4's own sentence; the line gives it a fixed place instead of a banner that appears. Presence rides here — who is at the desk, who is aboard — which is One hand's K in one line of mono, with no chips on rows. It replaces the connectivity pill, the freshness pill, the offline banner and the shell-version banner: four components, one line.
4 · Digits and words · what an instrument says, and how it holds still

Fixed digits

T−00:20, never T−0:20 — a countdown holds its width as it rolls, and shows seconds only under five minutes: T−04:59. 06:40, never 6:40 — the clock, the times in a plan, the hour a boat came home. 10/12 with the slash and the whole in meta — booked of seats, here of booked, aboard of expected, home of aboard. $41,180 — money as a figure with no cents when whole; $95.00 when a single payment is the subject.

The words

Lines off, not "departs" — the moment the boat leaves the dock, in the crew's words. Home, not "all home" with a hand sign — the count is the reassurance. Not aboard · Not back — the roll call's two red words, unchanged. Blocked · Attention — the two slot words with an ink; everything else in the slot is a count or a settled word in meta. No greeting. The page's name is the day; the day's name is the date.

Motion, complete

A digit rolls when its value changes — the countdown every minute, a count when someone checks in. Nothing from nowhere's roll, and the only motion. A gauge segment fills in the same beat as its digit rolls; it never animates on load. A press sinks a cell in the frame the finger lands, and springs back. Nothing else moves. No swell, no settle, no fold, no stagger, no reveal.

What this adds to the sheet

Two parts — the display and the gauge — and one line. The sheet's six parts become seven, and the meter narrows to counts that are not seats. Nothing is removed from round 3; the plain readout becomes the display's content.

What it still refuses

A second display on one surface. A gauge whose colour is not repeated in words beneath it. A telemetry line that hides a stale reading behind a friendlier phrase. A drawing in an empty state. Any hue beyond the seven.

What it needs

The display and the gauge as two components; the telemetry line as one, fed by the readers ConnectivityStatus and OfflineFreshnessPill already run; the four components it replaces deleted with their tests; fixed-digit formatting in format.ts.
