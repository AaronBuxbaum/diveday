A departure as an instrument, and the week as seven gauges

A boat's page is where the four trip tabs — Overview, Guests, Manifest, Prep — become one reading: the countdown on glass, the seat gauge, the boat's figures, its plan as a line of times, its lines with their states, and the roster as cells. The roll call opens from its one primary. The week is seven columns of gauges: a glance says which days are full and which need selling.

The boat's page

One display (lines off in 00:20), one gauge, five figures, five lines, ten cells, one button. Everything the four tabs held, on one screen, in the order a captain asks for it.

The week

A column per day; in each, the day's seats sold of seats sailed as a figure, a gauge per boat, and the boat's own line. Today's column is the one in ink. Reports' month is this page scrolled back.

What both delete

The trip layout's tab strip and its four pages; the board's drawn tiles, "+ Add" buttons and three header buttons; the "No price set" warnings as glyphs — a boat without a price is a line with an amber word.

The board, read at a glance. Ink is sailed and home; grey is booked and not yet here; red is a seat that cannot board or a boat with no crew; amber is a seat nobody has sold. Sunday's empty column ends in "Add a boat" — the last line of the list, as everywhere. Reports' month is this page scrolled back four weeks with the money figure at the top.

Where it stops

A shop with five boats a day gets five gauges in a column, which is honest and tall; the week then scrolls. The plan line assumes the crew taps stages (One hand's L); without those taps it shows the schedule and says "planned" in the slot.

What it needs

The trip layout's four pages become one; the roster reuses the counter's cell; the board's tiles, "+ Add" and header buttons go; the day gauge is the seat gauge stacked per boat. The week's readers are the board's.

Its wow

An owner sees the whole week as seats — which days are full, which need a deal — without opening a boat. A captain opens the 7:00 and the first thing on the page is the twenty minutes she has.
