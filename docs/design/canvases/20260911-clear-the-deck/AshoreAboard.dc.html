Ashore and aboard

The staff surface splits into two tools that share their data and nothing else. Ashore is the desk: a dense desktop ledger for the owner and the front desk — the week, the people, the money, the shop — read with a mouse and a keyboard. Aboard is the boat: a phone with one boat on it — who is here, who cannot board, the head count, the log — in type you can read in glare with a wet thumb. Nothing is drawn for both.

The case

Every staff surface today is drawn for a 13-inch laptop and a wet phone at once, and serves neither: the 44px floor bloats the desk, and the desk's density crowds the phone. On any screen, half the controls belong to the other place. Split by place and each tool keeps half the controls, by construction — the boat has one thing to do and the desk never scrolls past a roll call.

The honest tradeoff

Two things to learn, two to build, two to keep in step. The owner who runs the desk and then drives the boat switches tools mid-morning. "Which one has X?" is a new question. The counter — an iPad on the desk at 6:15 — is Aboard on a tablet, which is right and not obvious. And two systems drift unless they are one kit at two densities.

Cost

6–8 sessions. One kit, two densities (14px/40px ashore, 20px/64px aboard). Boat mode, glare mode and the phone dock are deleted: Aboard is boat mode. The manifest, the counter, the prep ticket and the log move aboard; everything else ashore. Composes with 1, 2 or 4 as Ashore's shape, and is the boat half that 6 needs.

What goes aboard

  • The counter, the manifest, the roll call, the prep ticket, the log — one boat at a time, opened from the boat's name. On a tablet at the desk it is the counter; on a phone at the rail it is the roll call; the same screen.
  • Nothing else. No board, no search, no settings, no messages, no money. A crew member who needs the desk walks to it.

What stays ashore

  • The day and the week, people, money, the shop's things and its card. Ashore may be drawn as Three words, The week or Ask — this concept decides the split, not the desk's shape.

What leaves

  • Boat mode and glare mode as switches (Aboard is both, always); the phone dock; the More sheet; the responsive fork on every staff page; the 44px floor on the desk (it holds aboard, where it was always for).

What it keeps

  • The roll call commits before it renders; a tap that undoes itself; the count that will not round up; never colour alone — the two who cannot board carry a sentence, not a hue.

Where it stops

The owner who is also the captain. Dana runs the desk at 6:15 and drives Skiff at 7:30; she opens Ashore on the iPad and Aboard on her phone and the app never pretends those are one thing. Parity questions ("can I fix a waiver from the boat?") get one answer: no — you can see that it is missing, and the desk fixes it.

What it needs

One kit, two densities. The same tokens, the same row, the same words, at 14/40 and 20/64. boat-mode and glare-mode become Aboard's only palette; StaffTabBar, the More sheet and the responsive breakpoints on /shop/** are deleted. Aboard is the offline-first surface, which the manifest viewer already is.

Its wow

The captain's phone has one boat on it and nothing else — no tab bar, no search, no menu, a number you can read from the wheel. The owner's screen has the whole week on it and nothing she has to scroll for. Each person sees a tool that was drawn for where they are standing.
