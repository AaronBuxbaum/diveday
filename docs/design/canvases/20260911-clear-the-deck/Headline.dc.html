Headline

Four type sizes and two colours, and that is the whole system. The day's name is 56px, a boat is 28px in the shop's colour, everything else is 17px black; there are no lines but the two rules that hold a group, no grey, no fills, no chrome to speak of. It reads across a counter and in glare because every word is either big or bold, and it is the one surface here that nothing else looks like.

The case

Reef put hierarchy in boxes, tints and marks. Headline puts it in the type and deletes everything else, which is what a newspaper front page and a departures board have always done. The 6 AM read is the biggest words on the page: the day, three boats in green, the people in bold. Nobody has to learn what a chip means.

The honest tradeoff

It spends height. A 56px title and 28px groups push the third boat below the fold on a laptop, and 17px sentences with no muted grey give every fact the same weight, so the eye has to read rather than scan the long lists. Bold enough to be a taste, which is the point and the risk: an owner either loves the poster or wants it quieter by week two.

Cost

3–4 sessions. Four sizes replace the nine-rung ramp and check:type-ramp shrinks to them; every tint, line, border, shadow and mark goes; one face replaces Geist; the phone gets 22px names and a 64px count from the same four sizes.

What stays of Reef

  • Nothing on a staff surface; the postcard on the recap until call (g). Harbor keeps the storefront in the shop's colour and face — and on Headline the storefront's boats are already 28px words in that colour, so the two sides rhyme.

What leaves

  • Every hue but ink, the shop's and red — including muted grey; the nine-rung type ramp; the radius ladder; the bed; the band, the tile, the dial, the chip, the pill, the glyph; every hairline; the eyebrow; Geist.

What it keeps

  • The safety floor and then some: 22px names and a 64px count on the phone, black on white, the two who cannot board in a red bold sentence with a green verb. The row is the target; the settled state is the word Here.

Where it stops

The long list. Without a muted grey every sentence is as loud as its neighbour, and a 20-diver roster is a wall of 17px. The answer is the group rule and the bold name, and it holds to about fifteen rows; past that Salt or Slate scan better. The 56px title is a taste an owner has to want.

What it needs

Four sizes, two colours, two rules. typography.ts shrinks to four constants; --muted is deleted, not dimmed; every line, fill and shadow class on a staff surface goes; one face replaces Geist. The storefront's group headings take the same 28px in the shop's colour.

Its wow

The day reads from across the shop. Three green boats, the people in bold, the count at the top of the counter's screen in numbers a captain can read from the wheel — and not one box, line or icon anywhere. It looks like a poster of the day, because that is what it is.
