Clear the deck, round 4 — Console, further

Round 3 drew Console: DiveDay as the shop's instrument, exact and undecorated. The owner's read: can we improve this further? This page takes "further" along the instrument's own axis — more instrument, not more decoration — and answers in three parts: five additions to the sheet that make a reading behave like a reading, the home redrawn with them, and four more surfaces drawn so the direction is proven where the shop actually works: a boat, the week, a person, the money, and the outside.

"Can we improve this further?"

What "further" means for an instrument

An instrument is not a quieter page; it is a thing with a display, a scale, a reading and a time. Round 3 had the reading and the words. What it lacked, read against a dive computer or a boat's console: the reading did not sit on a display, the count was a bar rather than a scale, nothing said when the reading was taken, an empty reading had no idiom, and the digits changed width as they rolled. Each is fixed below, and each is a thing every instrument has always had.

What it still refuses

A second display on one surface. A gauge whose colour is not said in words beneath it. A telemetry line that softens a stale reading. A drawing in an empty state — dashes, and a sentence. Any hue beyond the seven. A moment. A greeting. A photograph on a staff surface. Everything round 3 refused, still refused.

The boards on this page

The parts: the display, the seat gauge, the telemetry line, the digits and the words, and motion, each as a specimen with why it earns its place. The home, refined: Thursday at 6:40 with the five additions on it, 80px shorter than round 3's. A boat's page and the week: the 7:00 as one instrument, and August 24–30 as seven columns of gauges. A person and the money: Grace Mensah's record with the fix as its primary, and Shop › Money as the till on one page. The outside: the storefront's row in the shop's brand, the log card, and DiveDay's hero with the visitor's boat on glass.

The recommendation

Stated so it can be disagreed with: all five additions, and the four surfaces as the order to build after the home. The display and the gauge are what make a person say "instrument" rather than "clean list", and they cost one component each. The telemetry line is the safety floor's freshness rule given a permanent place, which is worth more than it looks. The boat's page is where the shop lives and the first surface to build after the home; the week second; the person and the money third; the outside last, because Harbor already holds it and the log card can wait for the crew's log.

The owner's calls, round 4 (H-73)

(m)The display: the one reading on dark glass by day, or round 3's plain figure. Recommended the display, one per surface. (o)The telemetry line on every surface, or only where a reading can be stale — the boat and the counter. Recommended every surface: an instrument that only sometimes says when it read is one you learn not to trust.

What no round changes

The name and the mark. Harbor on the diver's side, on Console's parts. The dock test: on every board here the red seats have red words beneath them and the one not aboard carries the minutes left. The roll call commits before it renders. Medical answers on no page. H-02. The claims policy: the hero shows the instrument and claims nothing it cannot do today.
