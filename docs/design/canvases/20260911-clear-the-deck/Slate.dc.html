Slate

A cool grey ground, one white sheet on it, hairlines, and a face designed for people who read badly in bad light. Fourteen-pixel rows at 44px, tabular figures, no fills, no marks, nothing warm. The shop's colour is the current word and the verb, red is the word Blocked, and everything else is ink on white. It is the surface that argues DiveDay is a tool first and should look like a good one.

The case

Reef was drawn to feel like a day on the water; Slate is drawn to be read at the counter with a queue in front of you. It is the densest surface here without being a table, and the face — Atkinson Hyperlegible, cut for low vision and glare — is the dock test as a typeface rather than a rule. A five-boat Saturday fits above the fold.

The honest tradeoff

It is the surface most likely to be called "another app". Grey ground and white sheets are what every admin tool wears, and nothing on Slate would make a shop owner say wow. The warmth is entirely the shop's colour on two things. And the compact rows that suit the desk are the wrong size for a phone, so the phone gets its own 48px rung, which is a second density to keep in step.

Cost

3 sessions. Tokens to seven; one radius (4); one sheet replaces the panel and the bed; the deletions as every surface; one face replaces Geist. The cheapest after Salt, and the most like today's tree in structure.

What stays of Reef

  • Nothing on a staff surface; the postcard on the recap until call (g). Harbor keeps the storefront in the shop's colour and face, on Slate's rows — which is the one place Slate looks like a shop rather than a tool.

What leaves

  • Every hue but the seven and red; the radius ladder; the bed; the band, the tile, the dial, the chip, the pill, the glyph; the eyebrow; the greeting at 44px; Geist.

What it keeps

  • The safety floor, and it is the reason for the face: at 16px on the phone, an l, an I and a 1 read as three different letters in glare. The two who cannot board carry a bold red sentence and a green verb. Boat mode is the same seven with the ink pushed to black.

Where it stops

The wow, honestly. Slate will never be the reason a shop chooses DiveDay; it is the reason they never notice the software. And two rungs (44 on the desk, 48 on the phone) is one more than Salt or Air carry — a small tax for a dense desk.

What it needs

Seven tokens, one radius, one sheet component replacing SectionCard, the bed and the panel; the deletions as every surface; one face replacing Geist, loaded once. The closest of the five to the tree as it stands, which is why it is cheap.

Its wow

A captain with reading glasses on a wet deck reads the roster without them. Everything is where a tool puts it, nothing is decorated, and a full Saturday fits on one laptop screen. The wow is that there is nothing to get used to.
