The shop's card

Every switch becomes a default. Settings — 43 rows on the hub and 12 more pages — collapses to one card of what the shop is (its name, colour, address, boats, crew, hours, money, the release it signs) and one line per thing DiveDay decided for it. A feature has no setting: it is on with a default a sentence can change, or it does not exist. Every control on a surface that exists because a setting could be off leaves with the setting.

The case

Each of the 43 rows spawned a conditional somewhere else — a lens rail that renders when lenses are set, a print register when print is on, a boat-line switch, a WhatsApp sender, a display page. "Far too many external-facing features" lives here: embeds in eight kinds, six print sheets, four integrations, a lobby display, calendar feeds. A shop leaving FareHarbor is not choosing a settings depth. Delete the switch and the surface loses its control for free.

The honest tradeoff

DiveDay decides more. A shop with a real exception — no online payments, a state's own release text, a boat that must never be public — needs a door that is not a switch: a sentence to support, or a flag DiveDay sets for them. Marketing loses "features" it currently sells by name. And "Change" is still a door on every decided line; the card has to be honest about which defaults are fixed.

Cost

4–5 sessions, mostly deletion — and every deletion has a test, a message key and a guard to remove, which is where the sessions go. The card is one page; each default is one line and one sentence. Composes with every other concept: it is Three words' Shop, The week's drawer, Ask's "settings", Ashore's fifth word and One face's strip.

What leaves the surfaces, not just the hub

  • On the storefront: the lens rail and its two selects, the season band, "Other ways we can help" (the date request stays as one line under the week), the badge wall's editor.
  • On the home: "Print the day" (the day prints), the season row, the lobby-display door.
  • On the boat: the boat-mode and glare switches, the haptics toggle, the text-size setting — the phone's own.
  • In the chrome: the language picker (the phone's language), the identity menu's four rows.

What it keeps

  • Nine facts a shop genuinely owns, edited where they are read. Three Connects. Two Copy-its. One Download. The waiver release as a versioned fact with a person's name on the version. H-02's retention and erasure promises, which were never a setting.

Where it stops

The genuine exception. A shop that must not take cards online, a state whose release is not Florida's, a boat that is never public — each is one flag DiveDay sets from a sentence the shop writes, and the card says "set by DiveDay for you on Aug 12". That door is support, and support is a person; the concept is honest that it moved a switch to a human.

What it needs

One page, nine editable facts, twelve sentences — and then the sweep: each retired row's guard, test, message keys and the conditional it drove in some other component. The embed catalogue becomes one snippet with one look; the print register becomes a print stylesheet on every page; four integrations become three connects. Most of the work is deletion, and it lands one row at a time.

Its wow

A new shop opens Settings and it is one card, already filled from the sentence they typed on the homepage — and under it, twelve things DiveDay already decided, each one a sentence a person would agree with. Nothing to configure before the first boat. That is the whole pitch to a shop leaving a tool with a settings tree.
