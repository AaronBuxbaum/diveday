Air

A warm-neutral white with no lines on it. Nothing is boxed, ruled or shadowed; rows are separated by air and lit by a soft fill only when a finger is on them. One humanist face at 16 and 34, ink and one muted grey, the shop's colour on the verb, red on the word Blocked. It is the calmest surface here, and the one closest to what DiveDay's brand words already say: gentle, never pointy.

The case

Reef's warmth was decoration; Air's is spacing and a rounder face. Nothing competes with the words, so the day reads at a glance and nothing on the page can be called ugly, because nothing on it is a thing — no band, no tile, no pill, no rule. The gentlest possible answer to "overbearing".

The honest tradeoff

It is the least distinctive of the five. Every calm tool of the last five years looks like this, and a shop owner will not describe it to a friend. Air also spends screen: 56px rows with air between them show fewer boats than Salt or Slate, so a five-boat Saturday scrolls. And with no lines, alignment has to be perfect or the page reads as loose.

Cost

3 sessions. Tokens to six; the radius ladder to one (12 on a row, 10 on a field); every border and shadow removed and held by a guard; one face replaces Geist. The hover fill is the one new thing.

What stays of Reef

  • The instinct, not the ink: warm rather than cold, rounder than square. Nothing drawn; the postcard on the recap until call (g). Harbor keeps the storefront in the shop's colour and face, on Air's rows.

What leaves

  • Every hue but ink, one grey, the fill, the shop's and red; the radius ladder; the bed; the band, the tile, the dial, the chip, the pill, the glyph; the eyebrow; every border and every hairline; Geist.

What it keeps

  • The safety floor — on the phone the row is a 60px block, names are 18px, the two who cannot board carry a sentence in red and a verb in green. The fill on a row under a finger is the press; nothing else moves.

Where it stops

Distinctiveness, and the long day. Air is the surface most tools already wear; its whole identity is the shop's colour and the words. And air costs rows: on a five-boat Saturday the owner scrolls, which Salt and Slate would not make her do.

What it needs

Six tokens, one face, one radius, and a guard that refuses a border. Every border and shadow class on a staff surface goes; a row's hover fill and its press are the one response. Boat mode gets stronger ink on the same six and keeps its 64px rows.

Its wow

You open it at 6 AM and there is nothing on the screen but the day, in a face that reads like a friend's handwriting. Nothing shouts, nothing is boxed, and the one green word is yours.
