The storefront's row, the log card, and DiveDay's own hero

Console is the staff instrument, but its parts reach the outside three ways: the storefront keeps the shop's colour and face on Console's line, figure and button; the recap becomes a log card that prints black on white with the dive's figures; and DiveDay's own homepage shows the instrument itself — the visitor's first Today as a live console — instead of describing it.

The storefront

Harbor stands: the bar in the shop's green, the day's name in its face. Beneath, each boat is Console's row: the time as a figure, the name, seats left as a figure in amber when few, the price, and one green button. No lens rail, no season band, no badge wall on the front page.

The log card

The postcard's replacement: a card the diver keeps, in the same seven tokens, printed or saved — the dives as lines with depth and time, the boat and crew, what the crew logged as seen, the next seat. No drawing; the figures are the keepsake.

DiveDay's hero

"Try it with your boats" already asks for a shop, a boat and a first departure. The hero draws the answer as a console: the visitor's boat on glass, counting down, with the seat gauge empty and waiting. The product is the demo.
