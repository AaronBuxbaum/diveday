Console — the sheet

Seven tokens in three schemes, two faces with one job each, five type sizes, one radius, and six parts: the readout, the figure, the line, the ladder, the meter, the cell. Everything on every staff surface is made of these, and a surface that needs more names it here first or does without.

The idea

An instrument shows the number you came for, labels it, and gets out of the way. Figures lead; words follow; a state is a word in a slot; nothing is decoration. DiveDay reads like the shop's console, not its brochure.

Where it comes from

Deck (09-01) drew the readout, the T−minus ladder and the roll call at depth; C (09-08) drew the state in a fixed slot and the two-component discipline. Console keeps all of that and drops what lost them: the dark desk, the cyan, the slot that held sentences, and the tab strip.

What it refuses

A drawing of anything. A tint behind text. A rounded panel. A second display face. A greeting. Coral. A colour that carries a state without a word beside it. A number that is not tabular. A moment.

Why two faces, and why these

Geist stays. H-64 kept it as the only face; Console keeps it for every word, so no new face enters for prose. Geist Mono for every figure, because an instrument's numbers align in columns and hold their width when they roll; the same family, so the two never argue. Five sizes: 56 (readout) · 28 (figure) · 24 (title) · 18 (name) · 16 (value); 14, 12 and 11 are the mono's clock, state and label. If Geist Mono's figures feel soft at 56px, IBM Plex Mono is the one alternative worth drawing; it is the call in (k). No display face, no greeting. The biggest word on any page is 24px; the biggest thing is a number.

Motion

A figure rolls when it changes and nothing else moves — Nothing from nowhere's roll is the one instrument-native motion and the only one Console keeps. No swell, no settle, no fold. A press sinks a cell in the frame the finger lands.

The diver's side

Harbor stands: the storefront in the shop's colour and face. Its rows and controls are Console's line, figure and button; its one readout per boat is seats left. The recap becomes a printed log card in the same system — figures, sites, times — and the drawing leaves it (call g).

What it needs

Seven tokens in three blocks replacing the palette, the washes, the night palette and the boat palette; five type constants replacing nine; six components — readout, figure, line, ladder, meter, cell — replacing the panel, the dial, the badge, the chip, the ledger row and the tile; the deletions of round 1's floor. Then the family-by-family sweep.
