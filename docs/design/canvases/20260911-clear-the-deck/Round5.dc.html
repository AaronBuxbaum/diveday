Clear the deck, round 5 — Chart: light renders light

Round 4's Console put its one reading on dark glass by day, and its whole day scheme was a cold grey with black ink. The owner's read: light mode should still render light, that implies further changes, and the surface should sit somewhere between an instrument and the existing design. This page answers with Chart — the instrument printed on Reef's paper: Console's parts, figures, words and discipline on the sand, shell, rope and ink the app already has, with the display lit, and the night palette the app already has as its dark. And it draws a dial: the same morning at three stops — Console, Chart, Reef after the floor — so "between" is a position the owner can point at rather than a word.

"I think light mode should render light still. This may imply we make additional changes and maybe make it somewhere between an instrument and the existing design."

What "light renders light" changes

A chart is the instrument that was always on paper: soundings in fixed figures, hairline contours, one compass rose, and nothing on it that is not a fact. It is read in daylight on a chart table and under a red lamp at night, and it is never a screen pretending to be one. Everything below follows from that.

What returns, what stays, what is new

Returns from Reef

The paper: sand, shell, tideline, rope. The ink and its muted. The two feedback inks. The night palette, whole. A panel — as the sheet, one rung, no bed. Warmth from the ground, which is where Reef always had it.

Stays from Console

Three words and Ask. The face, the figure, the line, the ladder, the gauge, the cell, the telemetry line. Every figure in mono at fixed width. Red and amber beside a word. The crew's words. Motion: a digit rolls, a segment fills, a cell sinks. Nothing drawn.

New on this page

The face, lit. One radius of 6. Glare as the crew's word, not the theme's. The sheet as the one panel, with a rule for when a thing gets one: a boat does; the desk does not.

What it still refuses

The greeting. The mark on a staff page. The water band, the dial's water, the horizon tiles. The coral on a staff page. A moment. A chip, a pill, a door. A second radius. A bed under anything at rest. A second face on one surface. A dark block on a light page. A gauge without its legend. A colour without its word. A drawing in an empty state. Everything round 4 refused, still refused — the list is shorter than Reef's and longer than Console's by exactly the sheet.

The boards on this page

The dial: the home's top three times — Console, Chart, Reef after the floor — like for like, with what each keeps and costs. Chart — the sheet: the nine tokens in light and at night with where each comes from, the shape, the parts lit, the same parts at night, glare as a choice, the words. The home by day: Thursday at 6:40 on the chart table — the face, three sheets, the desk on the paper, the telemetry line. The home at night: the same page at 6:10 PM in the app's own night palette, souls counted home. Three phones: the counter and the roll call in light, with the glare word in the roll call's bar, and the home in a pocket.

The recommendation

Stated so it can be disagreed with: Chart, as drawn — the middle stop. Eight of its nine tokens in each scheme are the app's own, so the sweep that lands it deletes the ladder's two rungs, the bed, the marks and the moments and changes no palette; the parts are Console's, already drawn on nine surfaces across two pages, and every one of them holds on paper without a change; the one new thing is the face, which is the display with its glass taken off. It costs less than Console (nothing repainted) and less than any of round 2's five (no new typeface). Its risk is the one the owner named in reverse: a shop that wanted the instrument's severity gets paper — and glare, one word at the rail, is where that severity still lives.
