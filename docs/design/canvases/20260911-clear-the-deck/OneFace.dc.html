One face

There is one DiveDay page per shop: the storefront, in the shop's brand. Staff sign in and the same page grows a thin strip and a layer of ink — the week divers see, with the counts and blockers staff need drawn onto it; a departure's public row opens as its manifest; Book becomes Seat someone. The staff app as a separate product — 57 routes, its own chrome, its own tokens — is deleted. Two things stay apart: the boat (Aboard, no brand, no public address) and the till.

The case

A shop looks at one thing, the thing its divers see, and fixes it where they see it. The storefront can never be stale because it is the desk. The design system halves: one component set, the shop's, and DiveDay's chrome is a strip. It is Harbor taken to its conclusion, and the one concept a shop owner would describe to another in a sentence: "our website is our office."

The honest tradeoff

Safety surfaces cannot wear a brand and cannot sit on a public address, so roll call, certs, waivers and medical stay a separate stark tool — this concept needs Ashore-and-aboard's boat half. Privacy is structural: the page a crawler gets must be byte-identical for anyone signed out; the layer renders only after the session. Money and reports have no public analog and live behind one door. And a desk working inside marketing copy will find the hero in its way by the second week.

Cost

8–10 sessions, the most of the six — the boat tool, the layer, the till, and the deletion of the staff app one family at a time. Composes with 5 (the card is the strip's Shop) and requires 3 (the boat). Ask rides in the strip as its one field.

What folds into the page

  • The home → the week, with the layer. Today's needs → the grey line under each row. Divers → the people behind a row and a course. Reviews → the review, with Publish. Courses, sites, the boats → edit where they are shown. Requests → "asked for this day" on the day. The board → the week itself, with "Add a boat" at the end of each day.

What stays apart, and why

  • The boat — counter, manifest, roll call, log — in DiveDay's tokens with no brand and no public address, because a head count is never decoration and never public.
  • The till — orders, Stripe, the month, the shop's card — one door in the strip, because money has no public face.

What leaves

  • The staff chrome, header, dock and More; ⌘K as a list; the staff tokens as a second set; fifty-five of the fifty-seven staff routes; most of the thirty-nine staff message namespaces. The storefront's front page also loses what the floor removes: the lens rail, the season band, the badge wall, the three disclosures, find-my-booking.

Where it stops

The hero. A desk does not want a 240px photograph above its morning; the strip has to fold it (one tap, remembered) and the concept has to admit that a page drawn to sell is a page that fights to be worked in. The crawler's page and the staffer's page must be one HTML document with the layer added after the session or the privacy promise is a wish.

What it needs

Aboard first (concept 3's boat half), then the layer: one annotation component in DiveDay's tokens, rendered only inside a session, from the readers Today already runs; the till as one page. Then the deletion of src/app/shop/** one family at a time, each family's e2e spec moving to the page. The storefront's composition tests already hold its length.

Its wow

A shop owner shows a friend her website on her phone, and it is also the thing she ran the morning on. There is no second app to explain, no "and then in the admin". The diver's page and the desk's page are the same page, and it is hers.
