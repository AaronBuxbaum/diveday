The week

There is one list in DiveDay: the calendar. Everything with a date — a departure, a booking, a waiver coming due, a request for a day, a blow-out, a close-out, an order, a unit due for service — is a mark on the week, and today is the column you are standing in. Nothing with a date has an index page of its own. The two things without a date, people and the shop's things, are one drawer.

The case

A dive shop already thinks in boats and weeks; the board is the page a shop opens first, and every incoming thing already has a day it belongs to. Requests appear on the day they ask for. Waivers come due the evening before the boat. Last Saturday is still there when you scroll back, logged — the shop's memory and its plan are the same page.

The honest tradeoff

Seven columns do not fit a phone, so the phone shows one day and you swipe. Work without a date — Grace's card — borrows the day of her next boat. A five-boat Saturday is a tall column. Reports become the weeks behind you, which is right for a day and wrong for the month's money; the month figure needs a home at the top of the past.

Cost

5–6 sessions. The grid is a new surface with hour rules and overlap. Eleven index pages are deleted with their tests, not hidden. The drawer is Three words' People and Shop as a sheet. Series become a block with a repeat mark. Composes with 5 (the drawer's Shop half) and with 4 (search over the week).

What folds into the week

  • Today, the board, check-in, the walk-in counter, Took a call, staffing, the blow-out, the close-out — the day column, today.
  • Requests — a dashed mark on the day asked for. Waivers — a mark the evening before. Orders — a mark at the minute the money moved. Series — a block with a repeat mark. Reports — the weeks behind you, with the month's figures at the top of the past.
  • Gear service, the season's events, a course's next session — marks on their days.

What leaves

  • Eleven index pages and their headers, filters, pagers and empty states: bookings, requests, waivers, orders, reports, staffing, calls, inbox, reviews, the close-out, the series list.
  • The board's three header buttons; "+ Add" under every day (the empty end of a column is the add); the horizons; the desk group as a place.

What it keeps

  • The trip page as a block's tap; the roll call as the boat's own screen; the drawer for people, gear, sites, courses, the team and the shop's card; ⌘K over the week ("sat" lands you in Saturday).

Where it stops

The month. A calendar is the wrong shape for "how was August" — the money figure and the year strip need the top of the past weeks, or Reports survives as one page in the drawer. The phone loses the week as a picture; the strip of five days is what it gets. Undated work borrows a day and says so.

What it needs

A grid with hour rules, overlap and marks, drawn once for the week and once for the day; every dated noun gets a day and a mark kind; the eleven index routes are removed with their tests; the drawer is a sheet over the week, and the shop's card lives in it. The storefront's week ledger and this grid read the same rows.

Its wow

You scroll back to last Saturday and it is still there, logged. You scroll forward and Emmet's request is already sitting on Friday, next to the boat it wants. The plan and the memory are one page, and nothing on it was typed into a form called Reports.
