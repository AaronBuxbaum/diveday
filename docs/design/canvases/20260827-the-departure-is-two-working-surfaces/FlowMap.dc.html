A day on the boat

The manifest's whole life, dock to dock. The crew moves the flow forward — nothing advances itself — and every beat keeps the same three promises: the count is huge, the alarm is earned, and nothing on screen can start a phone call by accident.

1

At the dock

Clear, then count

  • Blockers cleared on the Trip tab — held circles can't board
  • One tap per person as they step on; crew close the count
  • Boat checks tick down to zero
  • "Everyone's aboard" — the earned moment, then cast off
2

Dive 1

The screen rests

  • No timers, no auto-advance — the boat decides when to count
  • Offline copy holds the whole manifest; no signal needed from here on
  • The next checkpoint is offered, never forced
3

Back on the ladder

Count as they climb

  • Fresh circles for everyone who sailed; the dock's ashore carries over, dimmed
  • Tap = back aboard, with who-and-when kept
  • An open circle stays calm — mid-count it only means "not yet"
  • Count stalled? Mark the missing from their row's sheet, deliberately
4

If someone's not back

The alarm pins

  • The alarm exists only once a human records it — never before
  • The name goes to the top in red; the checkpoint stays open
  • Their buddy team is named — who to ask, first
  • The person sheet: the contact's number, today's trail, undo a mis-tap
  • The numbers (DAN, the chamber) wait behind ⋯ — the radio and O2 lead a real response

Found — re-tap settles it, back to stage 3's calm

5

Home

Close the day

  • Dive 2 at Winch Hole repeats stage 3, then the final count closes
  • Log the dives — one quiet door, surface-interval or evening work
  • Close-out happens ashore; the boat screen's job is done

The promises every beat keeps

Nothing is optimistic. A tap settles only once it's saved — a ghost count is how a boat sails wrong.
Checkpoints never advance themselves. The crew opens each count; the app only offers the next.
Only the dock's "ashore" carries forward. Every later checkpoint is a fresh physical head count.
Readiness gates only at the dock. After a dive, paperwork never stands between a person and being counted.
Re-tap undoes; the trail stays. Corrections are new events with who-and-when — never deletions.
Crew count too. A checkpoint closes only when divers and crew are all accounted for.
Works with no signal. The offline copy counts on its own and reconciles when the boat is back in service.
Emergencies are managed, not tapped. The radio and O2 kit lead a real response; the app's numbers (DAN, the chamber) sit behind ⋯ — reached maybe once a year, never in seconds — and the printed sheet keeps them all.
Consequence decides the gesture. Aboard is a tap and undoable; not-back is a deliberate two-step from the person's sheet; and there are no call buttons anywhere on the boat — phone numbers render as reference, safe from a wet thumb on a rocking deck.

Edges the flow already absorbs

Walk-in at the dock

Seated on the Trip tab; their row and a fresh circle appear in the count instantly.

Still blocked at cast-off

The held circle never opens; they stay ashore and carry forward like Georg.

Two phones counting

Both write the same trail; every result names its recorder, so a double-tap reads as two events, not a mystery.

Mis-tap mid-count

Re-tap reverses it on the spot; the audit keeps both acts.

Divemaster leads a group

Crew rows sit under the divers and the checkpoint won't close without them.

Buildable as drawn

Every state here already exists in the domain layer — checkpoints, carried-forward ashore, not-back semantics, split-team detection, divers-and-crew completeness, and the completion moment. The person sheet reads only data the app already holds. This flow is surface work, not schema work.
