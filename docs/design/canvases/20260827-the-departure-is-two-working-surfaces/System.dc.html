One person, one grammar

Four tabs become three: Overview folds into a Details panel on the trip page, so the departure has two working surfaces — the Trip page (sell it, clear it) and the Manifest (count it, on the boat). Every count is said once, rows at rest are a name and a mark, and what the rail doesn't need right now lives one tap away — or ashore.

The boat rule: three tiers

The manifest is worked one-handed at the rail. What earns the screen is what the count needs this second; everything else steps back.

Always on screen

  • Names, in manifest order
  • One 48–56 px tap per person
  • The count and who's left
  • The checkpoint

One tap away

  • A person: contact, medical, notes, audit trail
  • Emergency numbers and the response plan — behind ⋯, reached maybe once a year, never in seconds
  • Boat-check items
  • Buddy team editing

Ashore, not here

  • Clearing blockers — the Trip tab
  • Device settings, offline detail
  • The dive log — evening work
  • Anything that edits the departure

Paper keeps everything: the printed manifest still carries every contact, number, and advisory in full — screens hide, the sheet that goes ashore never does.

The roll-call tap

One circle at a fixed edge. Fill + mark + words carry the state — never color alone. Re-tap undoes; the audit trail lives in the person sheet and on paper. Consequence decides the gesture: aboard is a tap; not-back is recorded from the person's sheet, deliberately; and there are no call buttons on the boat — a real response starts with the radio and the O2 kit, so numbers render as reference.

Deliberately kept

  • Roll call is never optimistic; every result keeps its who-and-when.
  • Readiness gates boarding at the dock only — after a dive it's a head count.
  • The printed sheet keeps every fact the screen tucks away.
  • Blocked divers resolve on Trip, never mid-count on the boat.
  • The screen worries only with reason: an open circle mid-count is "not yet" — red appears once a human records not-back.
  • Emergency numbers are buried on purpose — behind ⋯ on the boat, one quiet line at the desk, always on the printed sheet. Reached maybe once a year, never in seconds; nothing on screen can start a call by accident.
  • Held for review: folding Overview and Prep's homes (one-home-per-action), and hiding the dock in boat mode.
