The logbook

Every diver already owns the object DiveDay should feel like: a ruled page with a date at the top, entries down the margin, a figure in the right column, and a stamp when something is cleared. So every surface becomes a page of the shop's one book. No panels, no shadows, no pills, no chevrons — margins, rules, type, and ink.

Why it coheres

One page grid does all the work: a 112px margin column (time, tag, or nothing), the entry, and a 200px right column (a figure or one verb). Today, gear, orders, divers, the manifest and the printed log are the same page, because the manifest was always going to be printed anyway.

What it deletes

Every rounded panel and its bed shadow, the 28/18/10 radius ladder, every capsule, the chevron, the coloured eyebrow, the filter chips (they become a running index line), the stat tiles, and the drawn creatures on staff pages — the hand moves to the diver's postcard, which is where it was earning its keep.

The honest tradeoff

Paper is a light-mode idea; the night page is slate, and it will be the second-best version. A serif at 19px in glare is a real question, so boat mode stays sans and stamps-only. And it is a fifth look in three weeks — this one has to be the last.
