Together — the app knows who else is in it

A dive shop is three to five people who spend the morning texting each other: "did Priya sign?", "who has the Spiegel Grove crew?", "I've got Diego". DiveDay holds every one of those facts and shows none of the people. This lever adds them: who is in the shop right now and where (Dana at the desk, Keiko on Mantis II, Sal at the counter), a small chip on the row a colleague is acting on, a one-tap hand-off on any Need, and the row that settles under your eyes when someone else settles it. Nothing else on the page changes. It is the lever that makes the app feel like a crew rather than a form.

Why it coheres

One chip — a person's initial and a verb — in three places: the header (who is here), a row's trailing slot (who is on it), and a settled row's sentence (who did it). The chip is the only new component, and it carries a person and a place, never a row's state; the row's mark still does.

What it changes in A

The header gains a "here now" strip. A Need's door gains "hand to …" as a second, quieter action. The counter's rows update live as a colleague checks people in, and the swell already built for a finished station now fires for everyone looking at it, not just the person who cleared it.

The honest tradeoff

A live layer the app does not have: presence and row-level events over a connection, with the offline manifest's discipline (a lost connection shows "as of 6:31", never a stale "here"). Who-is-looking is a privacy line: presence shows place and act, never a screen, and keeps no history. Safety rows never move under a finger — the roll call stays commit-then-render.
