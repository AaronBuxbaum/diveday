The tide line — the day on every page

One 64px strip under the chrome, on every staff page: the day from first light to the last boat as a line, each departure a block filled to its bookings, the next one lit, the boat that has sailed settled green (after 10:30 here — both drawings are before the first boat leaves), and a mark that moves with the clock. The page beneath is A; the strip takes over three things A said in words. It is the one thing a shop looks at forty times a day — how long until the lines come off — and today they open Today to find it. Here it is wherever they are, and it is what makes the app look like nobody else's from across the room.

Why it coheres

One instrument, shared. The eyebrow says which area you are in; the tide line says what time it is in the shop. Together they are the whole of a page's orientation, and neither is drawn twice.

What it changes in A

The greeting's lede loses its "before 7:00" clause — the line carries it. The counter's departure switcher becomes a tap on the line's blocks. Reef's water band by the hour becomes the line's wash, so the band stops being decoration and starts being the axis.

The honest tradeoff

64px on a phone is a real cost; the line folds to 32px with the "now" mark and the next boat only. A shop that runs one boat a day gets a mostly empty line, so the strip renders nothing on a day with no departures rather than an axis with nothing on it.
