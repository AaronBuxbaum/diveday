The wall, the sites, the courses

Gear, dive sites and courses are the shop's shelves: things the shop owns and keeps, not people and not days. The kit gives each shelf one list in the row grammar, grouped by state — out, reserved, needs service, on the wall — with the catalogue as a view of the same list, never a second page. An editor is the form primitives in two columns with what the diver reads beside them. New here: the wall in the empty state (P), the service clock as the one bar, and the site editor previewing the diver's briefing block as it is typed.

The one idea

Where every unit is, said once; what every site demands, said once; what every course costs, said once. A shelf's groups are its states, so the page has no filter chips and no stat tile, and a healthy unit says nothing at all.

Deleted here

The three stat tiles and the Returns panel on Gear, the service sentence on healthy units, "Add gear" in the header (the add row is last), the catalogue as a second page (a quiet row that opens a view of the same list), the site editor's own layout and rail (it is the form primitives in two columns; its eleven sections fold to summary rows), the course editor's rail and eight fieldsets, the per-row Schedule and Hide acts on Courses, the words Beginner and Intermediate (Easy and Moderate are what the crew says).

New here

P, the wall drawing. The unit's service clock as one bar. The site editor's preview: the trip page's briefing block — the photo, the sentence, the species with their pictures — redrawn as it is typed. The course editor previews the public course head in the shop's face. Four students on the Open Water row as stacked avatars.

Booked through DiveDay

The same editor shape as the site: fields in one column, the sessions as rows with the add row last, the deeper sections as summaries, one Save. The right column is the public course head in Bricolage Grotesque, the way a diver on the storefront sees it; the class dates on it come from the sessions on the left. The minimum age and the prerequisite come from the agency and stay read-only chips.

What does not change

Needs service is a pill and a unit off the wall never reaches a packing list. A site's certification requirement and maximum depth travel with it into every trip and still block admission at booking time. A course's minimum age and prerequisite come from the agency and are read-only. The catalogue never overwrites a shop's edits; an import makes an independent copy.

Phone

Gear folds to the four groups with the unit tag on the first line and Check in on the row; the unit is the reading column. Dive sites and Courses are the same rows wrapped once, the two metas beneath the name. Both editors become one column with the preview folded into a summary at the top ("What a diver reads ›") that opens a sheet.

Cost and risk

Sessions: Gear and the unit (one: groups replace chips and tiles, the service bar), Dive sites and the site editor (two: the list, then the preview as a client render of the trip page's briefing block), Courses and the editor (one). Risk: the site editor's route map and the course editor's day-by-day blocks are heavier than a summary row admits; they open in place and keep today's components inside.
