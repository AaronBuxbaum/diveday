The instrument

The tool a crew looks at on a deck should look like the other tool they look at on a deck: a dive computer. Dark by default, one bright action colour, every number a figure, every state a word in a fixed slot, nothing rounded past 4px, nothing that floats. Two components exist — a readout and a line — and every surface is made of them. The shop's warmth lives on its storefront, which wears the shop's brand already.

Why it coheres

A readout is label · figure · unit. A line is time · who · state · verb, on four fixed columns. There is no third component, so there is no third look: the home, gear, orders, the manifest, reports and settings are the same two things at different densities.

What it deletes

Panels, shadows, every radius above 4, the sand, the water band, the drawn creatures on staff pages, capsules, chips, the greeting, the coloured eyebrow, the four progress meters (one bar remains), and glare mode — because this is glare mode, all the time.

The honest tradeoff

It is cold on purpose, and an owner at a desk at 6 AM may want warm. The light scheme (the surface) is the second-class citizen here. And Reef's three shipped moments — the swell, the fill, the postcard — are re-cut in monochrome or moved to the diver's side.
