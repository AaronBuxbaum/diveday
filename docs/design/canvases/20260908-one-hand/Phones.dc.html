Six phones — the fold of everything

A phone is not a second app. Every surface on the desk folds the same way at 390: the tide line to a 36px strip, the tabs into the dock, the row into two lines with its sentence beneath, the photograph to a 96px head. Six phones from one Thursday — three staff, one on the boat, two the diver holds — drawn so the fold can be read off any one of them and applied to the rest.

The one idea

Everything folds the same way: the tide line to 36px, the tabs to the dock, the row to two lines, the photo band to 96. The column is the reading width; nothing is hidden and nothing is truncated; a row is as tall as its sentence.

Deleted here

The phone-only layouts. The greeting that folds into the chrome bar stays; the second tab bar goes, the per-page sheets that duplicate the More sheet go, the sticky pill with the seat count goes (the fold carries the count), the chips that collapse to bare times go (the fold is the chip), the 48px round "more" button on the manifest goes (the sheet opens from the name).

New here

The fold rules, written down: the tide line at 390 shows the next boat lit, the others as grey, one now mark and three words; presence avatars sit in the chrome's right slot and the who chip stays on the row; the trip's line keeps its dots and times and shows only the current word; the pass in a wallet is the top band and the three facts, with the code full-width beneath.

The dock test
  • Every tap target 44px: the tap circle, the check-in button, the dock's five slots, the chips at 32 inside a 44 hit area.
  • Critical text 16px: the sentence, the name, the time, the money, the state word; the kind word and the meta are the only 13s and 14s.
  • One hand in glare: the primary within the thumb's reach, boat mode at 6.2:1, nothing that needs a precise gesture; a swipe never commits a count.
What stays desktop-only

The week grid on the board (a phone gets the day list and the fold); the settings rail (a phone gets the More sheet's groups as pages); the print packet (a phone gets the offline manifest instead). Nothing else has a phone-only layout, and nothing a desk can do is missing from a phone.

Cost and risk

One layout per surface, folding by rule, is cheaper than two: the phone captures in the visual suite become the same six routes at 390 rather than a second set of components. The risk is the fold hiding a Need under the dock; the page clears the dock by its own height, and the last row of any list is the add row, so the end of a list is always visible.
