Round 3 — every surface, one hand, and eight more things to say "wow" about

The owner's read of round 2: "I like everything you've done here and I want to keep pushing these concepts farther. Keep cleaning up, iterating, and forming innovative concepts that will make customers go wow. Make sure we're designing every surface of the app." So this page does three things at once. It composes A with the three levers the owner liked — the tide line (F), the shop's own sea (H), together (K) — into one kit and holds every board to it. It redraws every surface the app has, seventy-one routes on eighteen boards, each board naming what today's page loses and what it gains, so the cleanup is per surface and not a slogan. And it adds eight levers, L to S, each one thing on top of the kit that no earlier canvas drew and that a diver or an owner would tell someone about: the trip's own line seen from both sides, the pass, the postcard, the log, the room in the empty state, the desk that answers, the shop's card, first light.

How to read this page

The Kit first (the whole vocabulary on one sheet). Then the surface boards in the order of a day: Today, the counter, the board, the trip, the manifest, the drawer, the shelves, the till, settings, the chrome; then the diver's side: the shopfront, booking, the day, the messages; then DiveDay's own pages, the moments, and six phones. Each board's head says its one idea, what is deleted, what is new; each foot says what does not change, how it folds, and what it costs.

What round 3 does not do

It does not open a sixth vocabulary: every board is drawn from the Kit and a board that needed something new says so. It does not touch the safety bans (no photo, coral or motion on a manifest, roll call, waiver or payment). It does not decide anything: the ADR's decision 5 records the round and the owner's calls are at the foot of this board.

The audit, closed

Round 1 counted six jobs with three to twenty-seven spellings. On this page each has one: one width on every board, the eyebrow the group on every board, the count as meta, one row, one add, three pills. The counts are now zero by construction; slice 20a–20e is what makes them zero in the tree.

1 · The eight levers · each one thing on top of the kit · the case, what it changes, the honest risk, the cost
Lone line, two sides

The trip's own line — check-in, boards, leaves, the sites, the surface interval, back — drawn once and shown on the staff trip page, the diver's trip page and thread, the lobby board and the reminder email. On the day, now moves on all of them. The desk's "where is the boat" and the diver's "where are we" are the same drawing.

Extends the tide line (F) from the day to one departure. The trip page gets it under the photo band; the thread gets it in place of the step spine once the day starts; the board gets it under the current row. The stage word the crew sets is what moves it.

The line is only as true as the crew's stage taps; a boat that never taps "on the surface" shows a line that is behind. So the line says "as of 9:12" beside the now mark when the last tap is stale, and never guesses.

Mthe pass

Booking confirmed becomes a pass in the shop's colour with the site's photo, the boat, the slip, the time, what to bring and a code. The counter scans it; the diver's screen says "You're aboard" in the same second the desk's row settles. It composes the Wallet slice (18f) instead of standing beside it.

The confirmation page, the confirmation email and the thread's top all become the pass. The counter gains a scan (the device's camera, no hardware). The row on the counter and the diver's phone are one event, which is K reaching the diver.

A scan is a convenience, not a gate: a diver with no phone is checked in by name exactly as today, and readiness is never decided by a code. The code carries a booking id and nothing else, never a medical fact or a price.

3–4 sessionsplus 18f · the diver's wow · Boards: DiverBooking, StaffCounter, DiverDay, Messages, Moments
Nthe postcard

The recap is a postcard: front, the site and the date with a stamp; back, the two dives with their times and depths from the crew's log, the crew, what you saw, the crew's one sentence, the review as one line, and "next time" with a reason. An image to keep and to send.

Extends the 09-04 recap proposal and draws it in the kit. The crew's Log tab is what prints it, so the log gains a place and a reason. The recap email carries the front; the thread's after-state carries both sides.

A postcard for a bad day is a bad postcard: it never renders for a cancelled trip, a no-show, or a trip whose log the crew did not send, and the review line stays one line.

2–3 sessionsan image render · the diver's second wow · Boards: DiverDay, StaffTrip, Messages, Moments
Othe log

Reports becomes the shop's log. The close-out writes the day's entry — "Thursday, August 27 · 23 divers, 2 boats, 1 walk-in. Nothing left open." — the season is a strip of days, and the month's figures come from the entries. An owner reads a year of their shop in one scroll.

Today's close-out gains its last act; Reports keeps its five figures and loses its tiles; the entries table is new. The fact of scale (the 1,000th diver) is written into the entry, so it is found again.

A log that is written for the owner is read by the owner and nobody else; it must cost the crew one tap. So DiveDay proposes the sentence and the crew only corrects it.

Pthe room

Round 2's rooms live on as drawings: the wall in Gear's empty state, the drawer in Divers', the board in the Schedule's, the counter on the lobby display when there is nothing to show. A new shop meets the shop it will have; never a second grammar for a list.

Six line drawings on the one `Illustration` register, drawn in the hand's stroke. They render only where the list is empty, which is the discipline the good-news line already keeps, and on `/board/[token]` between boats.

Lever E's sub-call from round 2 is answered by this: the board does not replace the ledger on Today, and the lobby gets the room. No drawing on any boat surface.

1–2 sessionssix drawings · warmth for day one · Boards: Kit, StaffDivers, StaffShelves, StaffManifest, Public
Qask the desk

⌘K answers in the row grammar. A name gives the row with its state and its fix; a day gives the tide line; a unit gives where it is. The palette is the one place a staffer types a question, and the answer is the same row they would find on the page.

Shipped in part on 09-06 (the diver, the day, the departure answered with the fact and the fix). Round 3 redraws it in the kit and adds the unit and the day's line as answers.

A search that answers is a search that can be wrong: it answers only with a row that exists and never composes a sentence of its own.

Rthe shop's card

Settings opens on the shop's card — the name in its face, the colour, the mark, the boats, the people, the storefront in miniature — and brand edits repaint it live. The forty-two rows become one card and a rail.

The settings index gains a pane; the brand page's preview (already built for day and night) becomes the card. Every fact on the card is a door into its page.

A card is one more surface to keep true; it reads the same rows the pages read, so it cannot drift.

1–2 sessionsone pane · the owner's setup wow · Boards: StaffSettings
Sfirst light

A new shop's first Today is the empty tide line with one invitation: "Put your first boat on the line." Onboarding is the first day, not a wizard: three quiet rows (your boats, your colour, your first diver) that are doors into the ordinary pages.

The onboard door stays one form; the wizard that would follow it is deleted before it is built. The welcome email says the same sentence.

A shop that arrives with a spreadsheet wants the import first; the third row is that door.

(f) The pass and the scan.

Whether the confirmed page and email become a pass carrying a code, and whether the counter may scan it with the device's camera. The code carries a booking id only; a diver with no phone is checked in by name as today.

Recommended: yes to both.

(g) The log.

Whether the close-out writes an editable entry, whether Reports keeps its title (the tab's word) while the page becomes the log, and whether the season strip may show how full the boats were (a count, never money).

Recommended: yes, keep the title, yes.

(h) The line's truth.

Whether the trip's line may move on the crew's stage taps alone (and say "as of" when stale), or whether it moves only on the roll call's checkpoints.

What is honest about the pictures

Photographs are gradients standing in for the shop's own; the fonts are the real faces; the numbers agree across boards. A frame is one moment, so a page is shown at the moment that makes its argument, and the aside says which.
