The one page — no tabs, one scroll from now to the shop

The staff app has twenty-three destinations behind four tabs and a More menu. This lever deletes the navigation: the shop is one page that runs from what is happening now, to later, to the desk, to the shop's own things, with a rail on desktop that says where you are and a thumb-scroll on a phone that goes the whole way. A register — the 140 divers, the 156 orders — opens as a sheet over the page and closes back to where you were; search is the door to any one record. It is the most efficient shape on a phone and the one a shop owner would not have seen elsewhere.

Why it coheres

There is one surface, so there is one width, one header, one rhythm, one row. The sections are the old tabs in order of how soon they matter. The rail and the eyebrow become the same thing: the section you are in.

What it changes in A

The chrome loses its tabs and keeps the shop, the search and the rail. The greeting stays at the top of Now. Every staff page that was a list becomes a section with its first rows and a door to the sheet; every page that was a form (a diver, a trip, settings) becomes a sheet.

The honest tradeoff

Sheets over a page are a phone idiom; on a desktop with two monitors, tabs were better. Deep links change (a diver's URL still works, but opens the sheet). A staffer who lives in one register all day scrolls past Now to reach it — the rail is for them, and it must be one tap. The lobby display is unaffected.
