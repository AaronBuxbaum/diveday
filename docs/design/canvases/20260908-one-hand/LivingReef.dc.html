V · The living reef

The crew sees turtles every morning and the storefront never knows. A site page today has a briefing, a difficulty, a photograph and a name, and it reads the same in March and in August. This board gives the crew's log two columns and the site page one read. After a dive, in the Log tab that already prints the postcard, the crew taps what was seen from the shop's own species list. The site page shows "Seen this month" with dates and how often; the storefront's site cards say "Turtles on 9 of the last 10 dives here"; and the tide line carries sunrise, sunset and the moon as quiet marks, so the night dive's row can say "moonrise 8:04, full tomorrow".

The one idea

A reef page written by the crew's taps is the most alive page on the web for that reef, and the shop that dives it every day is the only one who can write it. "Seen" is a record with a date, never a promise.

Composes

N's crew log, the site editor's field guide and the marine-life catalogue (the shop picks its species there today), the postcard's "what you saw", F's tide line, the storefront's site cards. Two columns on the log; one read on the site page; sun and moon math for the shop's coordinates.

What it never says

"You will see." A count of dives that were not logged. A species that is not in the catalogue's words. A sighting on a manifest, a roll call or a waiver. A crew that logs nothing shows nothing, and the page is round 3's.

What does not change

The catalogue's words and photographs; the site editor's field guide; the postcard's "what you saw", which now reads the same taps; the safety bans, since no sighting reaches a manifest, a roll call or a waiver.

How it folds

The Seen group's six cards become three rows on a phone; the tide line's marks fold away with the tide line, and the phone's strip says "moon 8:04" in the until slot on a night dive only.

What it costs

Two sessions after N: two columns on the crew's dive log (species and count), one read for the site page and the storefront cards, the sun and moon math (a small pure function in `src/lib/`, no dependency, tested against the almanac), and the three marks. The call is (n).
