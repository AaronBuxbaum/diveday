Today, through the day

Today is the work, in the order the day happens. The kit gives it one column, the tide line under the chrome, and the departures as stations that fill with rows and fold into one settled line when the boat is away. The greeting says what needs you, the station says what, and the close-out at the foot writes the day into the log. The four desktop frames are one page at 5:48, 7:04, 3:30 and 6:10.

The one idea

The work, in the order the day happens. A station is a departure with its Needs as rows; when the boat leaves, the station folds into a check line and the next one leads. The desk holds what belongs to no boat, and the foot holds tomorrow as one row.

Deleted here

The "First thing" panel (the station's first row is that). The tinted orientation card. The stat tiles. The second horizon card. The standing crew line (presence chips say who is where). The Tomorrow and This week tinted boxes (two summary rows). The date in the eyebrow (Front desk is the eyebrow; the date is in the lede). The intent line, the coral earned-moment line and the turtle, "Generate log" on every station, the Open seats row and the recap editor inside a settled station, the demo banner.

New here

O, the log: "Close the day" is a form in a panel that proposes the day's entry, and the entry appears on Reports the next morning. K: presence chips under the greeting say place and act; a row's chip says who has it. The tide line (F) carries the day's motion, so the page needs no stage banner: the block slides out and the station folds, under 400ms, no coral.

Cost and risk

The tide line and the folding station read the day model the page already builds; the close-out gains a proposed sentence (a template over counts it already has) and the log table it writes (O's schema, one row per day per shop). The risk is the lede: it must be computed from the same rows the page draws, or the count and the rows disagree by 7:05. The fold is one transition on the block and one on the station's height; reduced motion cuts both.
