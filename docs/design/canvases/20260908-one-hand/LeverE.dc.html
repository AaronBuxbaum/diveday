Rooms — the shop drawn as its own places

Every dive shop already runs its day on a whiteboard behind the counter: a column per boat, names in marker, crew on magnets, a strip along the bottom for what the desk owes. DiveDay draws that board, and the other places too — the gear wall, the counter, the drawer of diver cards — each with a layout the shop already knows, all made of one card at three sizes on Reef's tokens. Nothing skeuomorphic: no wood, no marker font, no shadows beyond the bed. A place, not a picture of one.

Why it coheres

One card: a name card (44px), a work card (the same, tinted), a tag (76px, mono). One column head. One tray. Every room composes those; the difference between rooms is where they sit, which is the point — spatial memory is faster than reading.

What it changes in A

Today's departure panels become columns side by side rather than stacked; the desk becomes a tray beneath. The ledger row survives everywhere a list is genuinely a list (orders, inbox, reviews). Gear's list becomes the wall; a unit's page stays a page.

The honest tradeoff

Three columns need 900px; on a phone the board is columns you swipe between, with the tray under each. It is a second grammar beside the ledger, so it must win as the whole staff app or not at all. And the lobby display already wants exactly this board — if E is declined for the home, it should still be the display.
