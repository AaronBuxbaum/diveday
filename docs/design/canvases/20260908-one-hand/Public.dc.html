DiveDay's own pages, and first light

Everything a shop sees before it has a shop, and the one screen it sees first after. The marketing pages carry DiveDay's own hand — the lagoon, Geist, the three bubbles with the smallest in coral — and say one thing each: the homepage tells a day in order, Product makes the claim and proves it, Pricing is one number and what it covers, About is who and the rules. The doors ask for one thing. The new thing here is lever S: after "Open your shop" there is no wizard; there is the shop's first Today, with an empty tide line and one invitation.

The one idea

The tide line is the homepage's hero device: a fictional shop's Thursday drawn as the same strip the staff app wears, then the day told in four bands, each a sentence and a small frame lifted from the app itself. A buyer who reads the homepage has already seen the product's grammar.

Deleted here

The nine-choice hero and its phone mockup (one primary, one secondary, the tide line). The mid-page demo door on the homepage and Product. The stacked switching links. The moments band's abstraction sentence. Product's chapter nav and nine-group list (Pricing's checks are that). Onboard's h2 group labels, shop-link and timezone fields, and three reassurance sentences. "Welcome back" and the marketing nav around every door. Every arrow, every sentence on a door that is not the act.

New here

S, first light: the onboard door leads straight to the new shop's Today — a grey mark, the tide line empty with "Put your first boat on the line ›", the greeting "Welcome, Dana", and three quiet rows under "Start here" that are doors into the ordinary pages. Also the town page's rows in each shop's own face (H reaching DiveDay's own pages), and the link card as the storefront's own head.

Cost and risk

The homepage's bands are the app's own components rendered with fixed fiction, so the marketing page imports the kit and drifts with it — one session, and the mockup components go. First light is one empty-state branch on Today and the tide line plus three rows, one session; the onboard door loses two fields, and the shop link and timezone move to the card. The risk is the homepage's claim that its frames are the product: they must stay the product's components, never a copy.
