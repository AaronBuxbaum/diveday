Their sea — Harbor comes inside

On 09-01 the owner decided the diver-facing pages wear the shop's brand and the staff app wears DiveDay's. This lever moves the line: the staff app also takes the shop's colour for its lagoon — the chrome's active tab, the primary button, every door — through the derivation rules Harbor already has, and each departure's header carries a photograph of the site, the shop's own or the catalogue's. Blue Mantis is green; its app is green; Molasses Reef at the top of the 7:00 panel looks like Molasses Reef. It is the warmest lever here and, after the tide line, the cheapest, because everything it needs is built.

Why it coheres

One token set with one input: the shop's colour. Harbor's rules already derive the tint, the deep and the contrast floor from it, and the storefront has run on them for a week. The staff app reads the same tokens; nothing is drawn twice, and a shop that sets no colour gets Reef exactly as today.

What it changes in A

A 150px photo band on the departure panel's head, the site name over it; the panel's time and figures move into the band. The hand's site mark survives as the fallback when the site has no photo. The chrome's identity square is the shop's logo, which it is already.

The honest tradeoff

A photograph is a decoration, and the illustration ban's reasons apply: no photo on a manifest, roll call, waiver or payment, and none on the counter. A garish brand colour is the shop's own problem, bounded by the derivation's contrast floor. Photos cost bytes on a dock with one bar of signal, so they load after the words.
