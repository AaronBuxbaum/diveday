The briefing

Principle 4 already says the words should sound like a good dive briefing. This direction makes that the whole design system: every surface opens by telling you the state, in five sentence shapes, and the controls live inside the sentences as verbs. The kind column goes, the chevron goes, the right-hand action goes — the sentence carries all three. Lists remain for registers, but a list is a stack of sentences too. When the voice is the grammar, a guard can read consistency, which no guard can do for pixels.

Why it coheres

Five shapes and nothing else — State, Need, Fact, Offer, Done — each with a fixed order of who · what · one verb. Today's twenty-two action kinds are all Needs. Settings' rows are Facts. An empty state is a State. A sentence that fits none of the five is deleted, which is the copy-restraint rule with teeth.

What it deletes

The kind word and its 92px column, the chevron, the trailing action, every capsule, the eyebrow-as-area, the stat tiles, the horizon pills, and every hint that restates its heading. Panels stay for departures only. Reef's palette and the hand stay; the glyph is the one mark before a sentence.

The honest tradeoff

Prose is slower to scan than columns: a register of 140 divers is a stack of Facts, which is fine, but Orders loses its money column to the end of a sentence, and Reports becomes a paragraph with four figures in it. And the Spanish bundle must be written as sentences, not translated as fragments.
