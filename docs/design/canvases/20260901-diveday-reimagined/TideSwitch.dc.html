What it does, and what it hands back to you.

What FareHarbor does well

Guests book and pay online on a page that works, and you never touch a card machine for it.
Its distribution network puts your trips in front of hotels, concierges and resellers you would never reach on your own. DiveDay has no equivalent and will not pretend to.
Nothing to pay monthly.

What it leaves on the clipboard

No certification record. A tours booking engine has no concept of one, so the level a diver holds lives wherever you last wrote it down.
No dive-medical waiver that fails closed — nothing that keeps an unsigned diver off the boat rather than merely noting it.
Its manifest is a passenger list you can download. It is not a roll call you run at the rail, twice, in the sun.
No rental fit, so the sizes each diver takes stay in your head or on a whiteboard.

The fee

FareHarbor publishes no rate at all. Nothing monthly — instead a fee on every booking, added to your guest’s checkout price, for as long as you use it.

FareHarbor doesn’t publish the number; you hear it on a sales call. Third parties report it at around 6%.

Ours

DiveDay takes no cut of a booking. Checkout runs through your own Stripe account, and what you pay us is the same number in a busy August as in a quiet January.

$99 per location / month, all of it, every feature. No setup fee, no per-seat maths.
