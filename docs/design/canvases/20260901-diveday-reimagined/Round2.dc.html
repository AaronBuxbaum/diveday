Round two · 2026-09-01

Their brand, not ours.

The brief was to find the thing that actually sells, and to match the embedding FareHarbor already gives these shops. So we read what dive shops build for themselves: a strong personal identity, a creature mark, a wall of certifications, and photographs of their own reef — and then a Book Now button that hands the diver to a page wearing somebody else’s clothes. Round two answers with a fourth direction, Harbor: DiveDay wears the shop’s brand, not its own, so a shop’s colour, mark, one display face and photographs drive its public storefront and every embed on its own website, while DiveDay’s safety vocabulary — the signal colours, the ledger, the manifest — never changes.

Harbor

Direction D Proposed

DiveDay wears the shop’s brand, not its own — on the storefront and inside every embed on the shop’s own website.

The shop owns

One brand colour. Its mark. One display face, chosen from six. Its photographs.

DiveDay keeps

The signal colours. The ledger. The manifest and roll call. The coral bubble.

The honest tradeoff. A shop with an ugly brand gets an ugly storefront, and we will have shipped it. DiveDay’s own brand goes nearly invisible to divers — which is the point, and a real marketing cost.
