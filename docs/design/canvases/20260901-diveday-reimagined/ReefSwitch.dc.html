What FareHarbor does, and what it hands back to you.

What it is genuinely good at

Guests book and pay online without talking to anyone, on a storefront that works.

Its distribution network puts your trips in front of hotels, concierges and resellers you would never reach on your own. DiveDay has no equivalent and will not pretend otherwise.

Nothing monthly. It is an online booking platform for tours, activities and rentals, owned by Booking Holdings, and plenty of dive shops run their storefront on it.

What it leaves on the clipboard

Certifications. It has no concept of a certification record, so the card check happens at the counter, from memory, on the morning of the dive.

Medical waivers. A general booking engine was not built to hold a dive release, attach it to the person and the booking, and keep somebody off the boat until it is signed.

The manifest. Its manifest is a passenger list you can download. A roll call is a thing you do twice a dive, on a wet deck, and it is not a spreadsheet.

Rental fit. The sizes each diver takes live in somebody’s head or a notebook, and get asked again every visit.

And the storefront is not free.

FareHarbor charges nothing monthly and takes a fee on every booking instead, added to your guest’s checkout price, for as long as you use it. It does not publish the rate — you hear it on a sales call. Third parties report around 6%. We will not do the arithmetic on your season for you; you already know what a cut of every booking feels like.
