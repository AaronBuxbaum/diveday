# Reef — warm and alive

**In one sentence:** Reef warms the sand DiveDay stands on, opens the lagoon-to-coral range into a real palette, and adds a single drawn hand — line-drawn marine life and water motifs — so the product feels like a good day on the water, while stopping dead at the rail where manifests, roll call, cert gating and waivers stay exact and calm.

**Motivation.** A dive shop's day is not a spreadsheet; it is the best-looking job in a beautiful place, run on the ugliest software in the industry. Clearwater made DiveDay quiet. Quiet is not the same as *wanted*, and a shop leaving FareHarbor is not choosing between two ledgers — it is choosing whose product it will look at every morning at six. Reef spends its budget on being the one that feels like the water it is about, because that is a thing no competitor is even attempting.

**The tradeoff, honestly.** Warmth plus drawn creatures is one bad decision away from a mascot, and a mascot on a boat-manifest product would cost more trust than the charm is worth. The system guards it three ways, all mechanical rather than tasteful: **Fraunces never labels an operational fact** — no display face on a row, a count, a state, a money figure or a control, so the personality can never reach a thing someone acts on; **no drawing may appear on a manifest, roll call, cert check, waiver or payment at all**, and the switching page's three proof mockups are drawn deliberately bare to prove it; and **signal colours do not move** — success, warning and danger keep their shipped values and still arrive as glyph plus word plus ink. Coral gets a bigger budget (three sanctioned appearances per surface, up from one) and in exchange gets a harder ban: never a status, never behind reading text, never on a safety surface. The second tradeoff is cost — a drawn illustration set is an asset a team has to maintain in one hand, and a shop with no photographs is the case it must carry.

**Three wow moments.**
1. *The water closes over the work.* When the last blocker on a departure clears, its rows do not merely disappear — a swell draws across where they were and the station settles into one warm sentence. The morning visibly finishing is the feeling a shop pays for.
2. *The count that fills as they come back.* Each diver counted in raises the water behind an exact tabular figure; at full it settles and one coral bubble leaves. The fill is decoration at fixed opacity — the number and the word "All home" carry the fact, in sun or at night.
3. *The diver's day, drawn.* The recap a diver already receives becomes something they screenshot: the site in the shop's own hand, what was actually logged, the review ask underneath. The prettiest page in the product is the one a diver shows a friend, and it is the shop's cheapest marketing.

**Kept from Clearwater:** the ledger row (glyph, one word of kind, one sentence, one fix), group headers owning shared facts, counts as tabular figures rather than pills, badges only for the exceptional state, one chrome bar, one primary per surface, and the whole of principle 9.

**Thrown out:** the flat hairline-only surface — Reef panels are soft (28px radius) and sit on a warm bed shadow; the near-invisible timeline dot, replaced by a drawn site mark as each departure's marker; the neutral white ground; and Clearwater's one-coral-moment ration.

**Display face:** **Fraunces** (variable, `SOFT 50 / WONK 1`, optical sizing on), a warm old-style with soft terminals, paired with Geist for every operational word. Fallback: Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia, serif.
