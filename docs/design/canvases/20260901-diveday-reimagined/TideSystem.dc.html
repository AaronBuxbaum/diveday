DiveDay · design direction

Tide

Editorial daylight. A dive shop’s day, set like a field guide — wide margins, one confident voice, and nothing on the page that has not earned the ink.

The page answers, in a sentence.

Every surface opens with one line of display type stating what is actually true this morning. It replaces the row of stat tiles, and it is built from the same facts the rows carry.

Space is the structure.

Hairlines and whitespace do the work boxes used to. There is one card shape in the whole system and most screens never reach for it.

Coral is the smallest bubble.

One warm mark per surface, and only when something finished. A shop’s working morning is deliberately a screen with no coral on it at all.

Two faces. Fraunces speaks, Geist works.

Fraunces (Google Fonts, variable opsz 9–144) · Geist

Fraunces is a warm, soft-cornered old-style with real optical sizing: at 72px it is a magazine, at 21px it is a section heading, and it never turns brittle the way a high-contrast display serif does on a sunlit phone. It carries page titles, the briefing sentence, section headings and departure names — anything a person reads once. Geist carries everything a person uses: rows, labels, counts, controls. Fallbacks: Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Georgia, serif and -apple-system, SF Pro Text, system-ui, sans-serif.

Sunlit sand, one lagoon, one deep band.

The family is unchanged — sand, lagoon, coral — tuned warmer and one shade deeper for print-weight reading. Three values are new: wet sand for banded sections, open water for the single dark band a marketing page is allowed, and coral ink, the only coral dark enough to carry a word (6.2:1 on sand).

Measure, shape, elevation.

The grid

1440 desktop: 144px margins, a 288px rail, a 64px gutter and an 800px column. Reading copy never exceeds 62 characters. Section rhythm is 64px in the app and 96px on a marketing page. Everything else is a multiple of 8.

Three radii, and that is all

16px for the one card, 999px for anything you press, and a 1px square rule for the ledger. No third box shape exists to drift into.

1 · The horizon

The day drawn as a waterline: today’s departures standing on it at their real hours, the dashed line where the shop is standing now. It is the only illustration on the page and every mark on it is a fact.

2 · The ascent

When the last diver is aboard, three bubbles rise once past the count and the smallest arrives coral. 320ms, no sound, no confetti — and it is the only coral the screen ever shows. Nothing else in Tide is allowed to borrow it.

3 · The briefing line

Where every other product puts four stat tiles, Tide puts one sentence written from the same facts — and it changes through the day. At 4 PM it reads “Two boats out, both due back before dark.” On a clear morning it says so and stops.
