Deck.

The whole product speaks the grammar of the boat’s own instruments: figures first, hairlines for structure, nothing decorative on the glass.

Dark at depth is the default, because the shop reads this at 6 AM on a dock and at 11 PM on a night boat. Sunlit is the alternate, not an afterthought.

Dense, never cluttered. Every pixel spent buys a fact a staffer would otherwise have to go and find.

Every surface leads with its number.

The count is not a statistic on a screen — it is the answer the person came for, so it is the largest object in the frame and it is set in the data face with tabular figures. Beside it, always, the word that says what it counts and the state that says whether it is right.

Two faces. Words in Geist, data in IBM Plex Mono.

fallbacks: system-ui, sans-serif · ui-monospace, SF Mono, Menlo

Geist keeps DiveDay’s voice: open, modern, quietly capable. IBM Plex Mono is the instrument face — drawn for engineering documents, with numerals that hold their shape at 76px and at 11px, and a grid that makes a column of times, seats and money line up without a table. Mono is never used for a sentence a person reads for meaning.

Two schemes, one family. Depth is the default.

ocean · lagoon · coral, retuned for instrument contrast

Depth runs deeper and colder than today’s dark theme so that a panel actually lifts off the ground and a hairline reads without glowing. Sunlit is the same structure in daylight, for the diver-facing shopfront and for a shop that works at a bright counter. Every ink pair below clears 6.2:1 against its own surface — the boat-mode bar, applied everywhere rather than only on the water.

Hairlines, not boxes. Corners barely turn.

Radius

0 for cells and rules, 3 for controls, 6 for panels. Nothing rounder — a soft corner reads as a consumer app, not an instrument.

Spacing

4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 56. Rows are 52px, controls 44px, primary controls 48px. Density comes from short gaps, never from small type.

2 · T−minus

The day is a countdown, not a calendar. Every departure carries the time left until the lines come off and the number of people who cannot board yet — so the order of work is decided before anyone has read a word.
