Design direction · one of three

Reef

Warm and alive. The same water, a warmer shore, and a drawn hand that stops at the rail.

Reef keeps DiveDay’s water and warms the sand it sits on: one family from lagoon to coral, used with intent instead of rationed down to nothing.

The hand is soft — wide radii, a swell line where other systems put a rule, and drawn marine life in one consistent stroke — so a shop’s day looks like a day on the water.

And it stops at the rail. Manifests, roll call, cert gating and waivers keep Geist, exact figures and a glyph beside every word. A boat is not a place for personality.

Two faces, one job each.

Where a drawing may appear

The storefront and the marketing pages. A course, a dive site, or a trip that has no photograph of its own. An empty state that is teaching rather than reporting. The diver’s recap. One earned moment on a staff surface, once a day. Never smaller than 20px, where the line stops reading and starts looking like a dingbat.

Where it may not

Inside a manifest, a roll call, a cert check, a waiver or a payment. As a status glyph — those come from the shipped icon family and nowhere else. More than one creature on a staff screen; the storefront hero is the single place a whole scene is drawn, and it is drawn once. Beside a refusal, a warning, or anything a person is about to be told they cannot do. And never animated on a work queue: a turtle that swims while somebody is blocked is the software enjoying itself.

The last blocker on a boat clears and its rows do not simply vanish — a swell draws across where they were and the station settles into one warm sentence. The morning’s work visibly finishing is the feeling a shop pays for.
