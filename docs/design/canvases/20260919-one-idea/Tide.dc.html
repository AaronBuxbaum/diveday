The app is the day

A dive shop's whole life is a run of departures under a sky, on water that moves. DiveDay already computes that sky — sunrise, sunset, the moon — and that water — the tide at every site a boat visits, the seas, the wind, the current — and files them under "Conditions" on the fourth tab of a trip. Tide makes the day itself the surface. There is no list of trips, because the day is the list: dawn at the top, the boats sitting on the hours they leave, the tide breathing under them, a line for now. Yesterday and tomorrow are a swipe away; the week is a pinch; the season is the year. Everything else is reached through when.

Feels like

Weather, Calendar, Fitness. The sky at the top is the shop's own hour — dawn at 6:40, blue at noon, dusk at 7:30, black after dark — so the page is light by day because it is day. Below it, white paper and the boats in order.

What organises it

Time. A boat is a moment on the day; a diver is on a boat on a day; a course is sessions on days; money is what the day made; a request is a day someone asked for, drawn as a ghost on the calendar. Only Settings has no hour, so it lives behind the shop's name.

Cost · risk

5–6 sessions. The home, the day strip and its almanac (all already in src/lib), the departure page, the storefront's front page; then each area re-hung on the calendar. The risk is kitsch: the rule is one flat gradient per hour and never a painting, white type at AA on every stop, and a plain black-on-white twin under glare.

What it deletes

  • The Trips list, the Board and Today as three places. They are one day, three zooms.
  • The four tabs and More. A date, a search, the shop's name.
  • The Conditions tab. The sky and the water are the page's top edge on every departure.
  • Dark mode as a setting. The hour decides; glare is the crew's word.

What it keeps

  • Every fact in src/lib: the day spine, the almanac, the tides, the forecast, the season, the week's seats — all already computed, none of it yet a surface.
  • The roll call's one tap, the manifest, the waiver, the card check: untouched, reached through the departure's hour.
  • Requests become days someone asked for, drawn on the week as ghosts.

Where it is weakest

  • A diver with no booking has no hour; the search finds them, and their record opens as a sheet over the day.
  • A shop without coordinates gets a neutral grey-blue sky — the honest fallback, one line to fix in Settings.
  • A pool session is a departure with no tide; the strip shows only the sun.
