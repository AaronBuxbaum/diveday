The app is the sea

A dive shop's territory is its water: a handful of sites a few miles out, each with its own depth, its own character, its own tide. DiveDay already holds every site's coordinates, its satellite frame, the route the shop drew over it, its briefing, its creatures and its tide station — and shows them one site at a time, on a page nobody opens on a dive day. Chart makes the shop's own water the surface. The home is a chart, north up: the dock at the bottom, the reef line, the sites where they are, and today's boats on their tracks. Tap a site for its briefing and today's water; tap a track for the boat. The diver sees the same chart on the storefront: where we go, and which boat has a seat.

Feels like

Maps, Find My, a chartplotter at the helm. Things are where they are. A new deckhand learns the shop's water from the home screen; a diver books the reef, not a row in a table.

What organises it

Place. Dives happen at sites; roll call at the dock; walk-ins, orders and waivers at the counter; courses at the pool and the classroom. Each is a place on the chart or a room behind the dock. Time is the sheet's order, and the week is the same chart with more tracks.

Cost · risk

6–7 sessions, one decision. The chart is drawn from coordinates the rows already hold — a site without them lines up in a tray until someone drops it on the water. The land is the decision: v1 ships the dock, the rings and the sites with no coastline; a public-domain coastline (Natural Earth 1:10m, bundled as one SVG per shop) is a follow-up ADR. The risk is a shop with one site, whose home is an empty sea.

What it deletes

  • The site library as a list, the trip page's Conditions tab, the storefront's site pages. One chart, tapped.
  • The four tabs and More. A chart, a search, the dock.
  • Every "where" sentence — the meeting point, the site name in a row, the travel time — is a place you can see.

What it keeps

  • The route a shop draws over a site (dive-site-route.ts) becomes the zoomed chart's own line.
  • The tide at every site is the label under it; the forecast is the corner of the chart.
  • The roll call, the manifest, readiness: untouched, reached through the track.

Where it is weakest

  • A shop with one site, or a pool: an empty sea. The sheet carries the day and the chart is a picture.
  • A site without coordinates sits in a tray until placed — honest, and a chore.
  • Money, messages, courses are rooms behind the dock: the same list every concept has, one tap further from the water.
