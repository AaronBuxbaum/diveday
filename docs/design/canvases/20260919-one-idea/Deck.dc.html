The app is the boat

The one document a dive shop cannot get wrong is the manifest: who is aboard, and who is not. The captain signs it; the coast guard asks for it. Deck draws the boat. Each hull is an object in its own colour with its seats laid out from bow to stern; a departure is that boat on that day, and its seats are the record — a seat fills when someone books, turns green when they step aboard, red when they cannot board, and stays open when nobody has taken it. Roll call is tapping seats. The count after the dive is the same picture. The home is today's boats, stacked like cards; the shop itself is the card at the bottom.

Feels like

Wallet, and a marina chalkboard. Objects you recognise before you read a word. A captain looks at the hull and knows the morning; a diver looks at the storefront and sees the empty seat they are about to take.

What organises it

The vessel. A boat has seats and a helm; a shore dive is a beach with a line of divers; a pool session is a pool; a course is the pool and a classroom. A diver is found through a seat or by search; money and messages sit on the shop's own card.

Cost · risk

5–6 sessions, one schema row. Each hull takes a colour once in Settings (boats.hull_color); seats are never numbered and never assigned, so no booking changes — the picture is the count made spatial. The risk is implied precision: a seat map that looks like a seating plan. The rule: no numbers on seats, ever.

What it deletes

  • The manifest as a table, the roll call as a list, check-in as a page. One picture, three moments.
  • Capacity as a number. An open seat is a shape you can see from the counter and from the storefront.
  • The four tabs and More. A date, a search, the shop's card.

What it keeps

  • Every rule in src/lib/readiness.ts and trip-admission.ts: a red seat is the same blocker, drawn.
  • Buddy pairs sit beside each other; the split-team alert is two seats that stopped touching.
  • The offline manifest is this boat, cached; it prints exactly as it looks.

Where it is weakest

  • A shore shop gets a beach — honest, less delightful than a hull.
  • A boat of forty needs seats at 18px; the picture holds, the initials go.
  • Money, messages and requests are not boat-shaped; they live on the shop's card, which is the one card that reads like a list.
