Who builds it

Started by an engineer who worked on Google Maps.

DiveDay started when a dive shop owner walked Aaron Buxbaum through a normal day at his shop. There was a whiteboard, a clipboard, a spreadsheet somebody updated by hand, and a few apps that didn't know about each other. He was paying for the software, and then paying again in hours he would rather have spent with his divers.

Aaron's background is Google Maps, a biotech company he helped build through to going public, and self-driving cars. All of it is software a lot of people depend on, and all of it has to get things right. DiveDay is held to that bar. The whiteboard made sense too. Paper doesn't crash, and it doesn't need five taps with wet hands.

We're building the thing we'd want if we ran the shop. It has to open fast on a phone in the sun and still make sense to a new hire. The four rules below are how we check that, and you can check them too.

Google Maps

The app your guests use to find your dock, and the bar DiveDay is held to for how fast a screen comes up on a phone outside.

A biotech company, built through to going public

Records that get audited. The habit DiveDay took from it is never overwriting anything: every roll call and every correction is kept.

Self-driving cars

Software that stops when it isn't sure. DiveDay's readiness check works the same way: if it can't verify something, it says so instead of letting the diver through.
