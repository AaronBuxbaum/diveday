Finding 3 · the support sentence, three ways

Who answers the email is the one support claim no rival can copy.

The same sentence lands in five places — the included list on /pricing, the “How it's run” band and the “Who answers you” row on /about, the closing question on /pricing, and the contact half of the homepage's close. Each candidate below is shown as it would render in two of them. One is recommended; the pick is the owner's, and it is H-66.

Option A · Today

A real person reads it.

Email support — write in, hear back from a real person

Who answers you

A real person on the DiveDay team — email support@dive.day anytime.

Motivation. Never over-promises, survives any team size, and was chosen on 2026-08-05 precisely because a founder line had read as a statement about headcount beside “one person owns every line of code”.

Tradeoff. Any rival can paste it. FareHarbor's pitch is 24/7 support by phone, so on “support exists” DiveDay loses on volume. It argues nothing about who.

Changes nothing. The rest of the canvas still stands.

Option B · Recommended

The people who build it read it.

Email support, read by the people who build DiveDay

Who answers you

The people who build it. Email support@dive.day.

Motivation. DiveShop360's support is tiered under private-equity ownership, EVE's is a back-office vendor, FareHarbor's is a call centre. None of them can say the people who build the product read the support inbox. It says nothing about headcount, which is what made the 2026-08-05 line read as a statement about size, and it sits beside the proof that the season does not depend on anyone in particular — the shop's own Stripe account, the ZIP, roll call with no signal.

Tradeoff. A promise about who reads the inbox, so it stays true only while the builders do. No response time, no day, no hour budget. Not a reversal of H-12: it names no individual and promises no personal answer — it sharpens the line the policy already allows, that the inbox reaches the same team.

Inside the current claims policy; still the owner's nod, since it is a promise about who reads.

Option C · A support desk

Support staff, on hand.

Support staff who know the product, by email

Who answers you

DiveDay's support team — email support@dive.day.

Motivation. Scales past one person, and reads as an established company.

Tradeoff. Not true today, so it is roadmap marketing until someone is hired — a claims-policy failure, not a wording one. And it is the sentence every rival already has: it differentiates nothing. Hire when the cohort needs it, and say so then.

Not a differentiator. Recommended against as the reason to pick DiveDay.

Why B reads as access, not as size

The 2026-08-05 retirement was right about the sentence it retired: “One person owns every line of code running on this boat” describes headcount, and a buyer has no reason to want a vendor with fewer people. “The people who build it read your email” describes what the buyer gets. The difference is the subject of the sentence. It also only works next to the facts that make the season not depend on any one of them — which is why on /about it sits directly under the hero that states them, and on / it sits in the close beside the price and the doors, never in the hero.

What none of the three may say

A response time or a day of the week. “Same-day”, “24/7”, “within the hour”. A support hour budget. A named individual anywhere but the founder band on /about — the support sentence says the people who build it, not a name, so the promise is about the role and can be retired without editing a biography. And never “I”: DiveDay is not one person, and a sentence that says “I” tells the reader it is.
