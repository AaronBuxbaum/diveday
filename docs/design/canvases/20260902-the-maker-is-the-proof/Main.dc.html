The maker is the proof.

An evaluation of /, /product, /pricing, /about, /switching and /onboard as they render today, and the changes it argues. Nothing on this canvas is normative: the ADR carries the decisions, two of them are the owner's, and code obeys the ADR.

What is working, and stays

The price hero — figure, negations, both doors, the included list, one screen. The two-doors rule (demo leads, trial follows) holding on every page. The four checkable rules on /about. The numbered breadth band and the mirrored records diptych on /. The concierge at the top of every move rail. The captions: “wet thumbs”, “a bad morning at the counter”, “better you hear ours from us”.

The pages are correct, calm and consistent with Reef. The findings are about what they leave unsaid.

Four findings

1

The site has no maker.

Every page argues the product's safety — safe to run, safe to leave — and none argues the person. “From the founder” carries no name. The confirmed background (Google Maps; a biotech company built through to going public; self-driving cars) came off /about on 2026-08-05 because a résumé block did not tell the story. Against a three-person studio, a private-equity POS and a back-office PC, the maker is the differentiator, and today's pages spend it nowhere.

2

The pages argue insurance, not desire.

The product's bet is delight, and the copy's proof is almost all exits and gates: the export appears on /, on /pricing, three times on /about and on every guide. Nothing says why this one will feel different — “fast, forgiving, opens on one bar” lives in a caption. The delight thesis needs a reason to be believed, and the credential is that reason. They are the same missing paragraph.

3

Support is described as a mailbox.

“A real person reads it” is on every page, and any rival can paste it; FareHarbor's whole pitch is 24/7 support, so “support exists” cannot win. What no incumbent can write: the people answering are the people who build it. That line was retired on 2026-08-05 because “founder-direct” read small — but it read small beside “one person owns every line of code”, a sentence about size. Worded as access, beside the Stripe-account, ZIP and offline proof, it is the sharpest support claim on the market.

4

The copy sounds machine-written.

Not wrong, and not vague — the opposite. Every sentence is a finished line: the “X — not Y” tail (“at the desk — not at the dock”, “a file, not a project”), the balanced pair (“The desk clears it in the morning. The captain sees it at the dock.”), the triplet for rhythm (“fast, clear, and forgiving”), the closing aphorism (“These aren't promises.”, “Better you hear ours from us than find it in your second week.”). One of these is a good sentence. Forty of them in a row is a tell, and a shop owner reading at night hears a machine, which is the one thing a page about trust cannot afford. The fix is not shorter; it is plainer — one long sentence then a short one that is not a punchline, a specific instead of an adjective, and a paragraph allowed to end on a fact.

Two calls only the owner can make

1 · The biography comes back, as a story, with the name in it. The three facts are already confirmed (2026-07-25). The band names Aaron in the third person and says “we” where it speaks at all, because DiveDay is not one person's side project and is not built by one person. The background is listed without a tense, so nothing says what anyone does “these days”. The three paragraphs and the three one-line glosses are drafts: confirm, reword or strike each one, and whether self-driving cars stays on the list is the owner's.

2 · Which support sentence. Keeping today's line changes nothing. “Read by the people who build DiveDay” names no individual and promises no response time, so it sits inside the current policy rather than reversing the 2026-08-05 retirement in H-12. It still needs the owner's nod, because it is a promise about who reads the inbox.

How “ex-Google” is used, and never used

In prose once, on /about, as “worked on Google Maps” — the product a shop's guests already use to find the dock, not a company name. Elsewhere only as a ledger label beside a product fact (the /about ledger, the / ledger) and a clause in /about's search description. Never a heading on /.

Never: a Google logo or any logo wall (it would read as an affiliation Google did not grant); “ex-Google” as an eyebrow or in a hero headline; “Google-grade”; a home location; a response time; a customer count beside it. The hero headline stays about the buyer's position — the maker is proof, never the pitch.

How to read the canvas. The cover and the support board argue; the About, phone and Home boards show the bands in the app's own tokens — Reef's sand, shell and rope, Geist at the marketing type ramp, the 28px panel — sitting among the bands that already ship, so the new one is judged in context. The ledger is the change list the code phase works from.

What every board keeps. The claims policy: shipped-only, true-only for biography, no fabricated proof, the price only from marketing.ts. The two doors in their order. One primary per screen. The concessions. And the apologetics test — no proposed sentence says small, new vendor, on faith, or borrow credibility.
