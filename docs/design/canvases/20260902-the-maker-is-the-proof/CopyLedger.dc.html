E · The voice pass — eight tells, one example from each page

The tellsapplied to every proposed line above, and to be swept across the live pages

1 · The “X — not Y” tail: “at the desk — not at the dock”. Say the one thing.  2 · The balanced pair: “The desk clears it in the morning. The captain sees it at the dock.”  3 · The triplet for rhythm: “fast, clear, and forgiving”. Two items, or four uneven ones.  4 · The closing aphorism: “These aren't promises.” End on the fact.  5 · “It's not X. It's Y.”  6 · An adjective where a specific would do: a time, a count, a name.  7 · No “I”, anywhere: DiveDay is not one person, and a page that says “I” tells the reader it is. “We” only for what the whole team shares.  8 · No self-aware asides, no zingers, no rhetorical-question headings (“Rather ask a question first?” is shipped and is one). If a sentence makes you smile at your own cleverness, cut it. The test is whether you would say it across the counter to the shop owner, in those words. And the dash budget: one per paragraph, at most.

The existing rule — read it aloud as a briefing — catches jargon and misses polish. The second ear is the one that matters now: would you text this to a friend who runs a shop, in these words?
