## Context

Fifteen design canvases in thirty days, and every one of them — this brief's two earlier rounds
included — took the app's skeleton as given: pages of rows under a nav of nouns (Today · Check-in ·
Divers · Board, fifteen more under More, a ⌘K box), a title, a list, a chevron on each row, a
filled button somewhere. Then it argued about the clothes: a palette (Reef), deletions (One hand),
six ways to group the nouns and five surfaces and a console (Clear the deck), a floor and three skins
and the device's own face (Nothing to explain). Each brief was answered at the altitude it was asked,
and none asked the question Apple asks first.

The cover puts the whole current app on one sheet: 57 distinct surfaces at phone width, the first
screen of each. Read as one thing, every page is the same page. Nothing on the sheet is a thing a
dive shop already knows — not a day, not a boat, not the water — so every screen needs its words to
say what it is, and those words are what the owner has been reading as "the design".

The count in the tree at `b49abbe`: 99 page routes (60 staff, 9 storefront, 13 by token, 17
marketing, account and legal); 21 staff destinations in one registry; 18 pages under Settings.

An Apple product is not a skin on a database. It is one idea about the thing itself, and the chrome
disappears because the thing is legible on its own: Weather is the sky, Wallet is the cards, Maps is
the map, Fitness is the rings, Clock is the clock. **The test is one sentence: name the physical
thing the app is.** If the answer is "a list", the design is not done, whatever it is wearing.

A dive shop already thinks in three physical things, and each can carry the whole product.

## Decision

### 1. What stands from 20260918

Every idea below is drawn on round 2's surface and on the parts of the floor that are not about the
skeleton, unchanged and by reference: one ramp on the device's face with Inter then Geist as the
fallback (22a); the surface — cool neutrals and pure white, near-black ink, one DiveDay blue in two
strengths, red and amber always beside a word, green only as the aboard fill, two concentric radii,
capsules, black at night, the shop's colour on the storefront only (22b); the icons re-cut (22i);
nothing drawn on `/shop/**` (22c). Those rows land first under any pick. The floor's shell rows
(22d–22h: one shell of four tabs, one row and one group as the unit of the page, the directions'
parts) are withdrawn, because the shell is the skeleton and the skeleton is what changes.

### 2. Three ideas, each a whole product

Each is stated so a guard can hold it. Every idea keeps the same six screens for the comparison
(the home in a pocket and on a desk, the departure, the night roll call, the storefront), the same
fiction, and the same safety rules (§4).

### 3. The decision (recommended, then chosen)

**H-88: I · Tide**, with the departure drawn as Deck's hull and a site's page drawn as Chart's chart
inside it.

1. **Every shop has a day.** Not every shop has a hull with seats, and not every shop has six charted
   sites. A shore shop and a course-heavy shop are ordinary customers; Tide is the only idea that is
   whole for all of them.
2. **The day is what DiveDay already knows most about**, and none of it is a surface yet.
3. **It answers the owner's reads** on H-77 without a toggle: light by day because it is day; a sky
   over paper is "between an instrument and the existing design"; a chart's discipline with no
   decoration is "farther from the cutesy".
4. **It composes in one direction.** The app is the day; on the day are boats; the boats go to
   places. So under Tide the departure is Deck's hull and a site is Chart's chart — the picture each
   thing already is — and nothing from II or III is lost. The reverse is not true: a boat-first app
   has nowhere to put a Tuesday with no boats, and a chart-first app has nowhere to put a pool.

II · Deck is the pick for a shop that lives on the roll call and reads the boat before the day; III ·
Chart for a shop whose water is its identity and whose crew changes every season.

**Chosen 2026-09-19: I · Tide**, as recommended and with both compositions. What that settles, so no
implementer has to re-derive it:

- **The home is the day** and the day is the only spine. A surface that is not a moment on a day is
  reached by search or from the object it belongs to, never from a tab.
- **A departure's page is Deck's hull**: the seats laid out bow to stern, a seat per **held** booking
  in booking order, never numbered and never assigned. `boats.hull_color` is the one column Deck
  asked for and it lands with the departure.

  Two corrections to that sentence, from the dive-domain review of the build (2026-09-19):

  **The length comes from `trips.capacity`, not `boats.capacity`.** Two departures on one vessel can
  therefore draw as different-length boats, which does cut against "an object a crew recognises
  before reading a word" — and it is still right: a shop that sells ten seats on a twelve-place hull
  for a course morning would otherwise see two places drawn that nobody may sit in, and a picture
  offering a seat that cannot be sold is a worse lie than a hull that is short.

  **"A seat per booking" is a seat per booking that still holds one** (`SEAT_HELD_STATUSES`, the
  glossary's *Seat held*) — not per non-cancelled row. Drawn the looser way the hull read full over
  a seat the counter could sell, and, on the ordinary morning where a no-show's released seat is
  bought by a walk-up, silently left that walk-up off the boat.
- **A site's page is Chart's chart**: the shop's water north up, the dock, the rings, the sites where
  they are. It is the last surface in the plan and its coastline stays a follow-up ADR.
- **The sky is a picture of the hour and gates nothing.** `src/lib/sun-moon.ts`'s "informs and gates
  nothing" holds verbatim: no readiness, admission, capacity or notification rule reads a scheme, a
  gradient or a strip. A shop with no coordinates gets the neutral scheme rather than a wrong one.

### 3b. What must close before a hull is ever a manifest

The hull today draws the *dock's* question — open, booked, blocked, and now "nobody looked" —
because the departure page has no roll call in it and every caller hands `seatReadingFor` a null
`recorded`. The recorded states are in the vocabulary, tested, and unreachable. The dive-domain
review of 2026-09-19 found that several things are safe only because of that, and they are written
here rather than left for the session that wires the manifest to rediscover.

**Four of the six are closed** (PR against `claude/tide-10-manifest-prerequisites`); each is marked
below with what closed it and, where a half remains, exactly which half. The two that are open are
the two that are about *drawing*, and they are the reason this list still stands between the hull
and the manifest.

A second dive-domain review of that PR found a hole this list did not contain: the checkpoint had
been added to the *recorded* half only, so on the path where nothing is recorded the derivation had
no checkpoint at all and answered the dock's question at every head count. An ordinary two-tank
morning reaches it — aboard at the dock, nobody tapped yet after dive one — and the seat painted
"nothing stopping them boarding" over a diver who may be in the water. Item 1 below carries the fix;
the lesson is that a list like this one is not a substitute for reading the derivation whole.

1. **The checkpoint is not in the derivation.** ✅ **Derivation closed; the sentence is not.** Green
   at the dock means "got on the boat"; green after dive two means "came back". Amber at the dock
   means "never left the dock"; amber after dive two is a search. The checkpoint is now a property
   of the *drawing*: `SeatPlace` is two arms, one for a surface with no roll call in it and one
   naming the head count being drawn, and a recorded tone lives only on the second — so handing a
   tone to a dock-only picture is a `tsc` error. Nothing recorded at an after-dive checkpoint is
   `awaiting`, and readiness is not consulted there at all, because **Held** says readiness "exists
   only at the dock" and a blocked diver counts back aboard like anyone else. **What is left**: the
   hull's own sentence still has no checkpoint in it. That copy belongs with the manifest that
   first makes a recorded tone reachable; `SeatReading.checkpoint` already carries what it needs.
2. **`SeatState` cannot say "aboard, and nobody ever cleared them."** ✅ **Derivation closed; the
   drawing is not.** Precedence is unchanged and right — a recorded fact outranks readiness — but
   the derivation no longer *discards* the readiness on the way through: `seatReadingFor` returns a
   `SeatReading` carrying `state`, `checkpoint` and `readiness` together, and `HullSeatContent`
   takes the whole reading rather than the bare state. **What is left**: nothing paints
   `readiness` yet. A hull that draws a recorded tone has to show that its green was never cleared,
   and designing that mark is the manifest's work, not a guess made in advance of it.
3. **`ashore` collapses a statement into an inference.** ✅ **Closed.** The rows give
   `notBoardedImplied` a quieter, dashed reading on purpose (decision 4 of ADR 20260827: an alarm
   is earned by a recorded fact, never by the absence of one). `ashoreImplied` is now its own
   `SeatState`, drawn dashed and on the tint rather than solid on the fill, and
   `src/components/boat/Hull.test.tsx` holds the two apart by both.
4. **The hull does not print**, and a manifest does. ⚠️ **Made harder by the work above, and worth
   knowing before starting.** `--warning-tint` and `--danger-tint` are not redefined in the print
   block, and `background: transparent !important` does not reach an SVG `fill` attribute — so on
   paper three states now collapse to "dashed, near-white" (`open`, `awaiting`, `ashoreImplied`)
   where one did before. The dash is the channel that survives flattening and this work spent it
   three times; whoever cuts the mono treatment has to re-cut three states, not one. The print palette flattens `--success` and
   `--warning` to one near-black and `--danger` to black, so aboard and ashore print identically and
   a booked seat prints white on white. A manifest hull needs states told apart by outline weight,
   hatch or glyph — not by hue.
5. **There is no "not known" seat.** ✅ **Closed.** `rosterRowIsBlocked` still fails open — that is
   its job elsewhere — so `TripHull` no longer asks it first: `readinessOf` checks the booking's
   absence from the readiness map *before* the predicate and answers `unread`, which
   `seatReadingFor` draws as `awaiting`. Not `unknown`: the glossary already owns that word for an
   aboard-blocker kind, which is a diver who **is** blocked, and the two fail in opposite
   directions. The seat keeps a **solid** line — a first pass drew it dashed, which on a hull too
   big to letter (every boat over a six-pack) made a booked diver nobody had vetted render as an
   empty seat — and carries its doubt on a slate fill and a dashed inset that both survive print.
   The count is said in words too, off the same three-way answer the seats are drawn from, because
   a dash pattern is colour's quieter cousin and design principle 6 covers both.
6. **A hull is not everybody aboard.** ⚠️ **More urgent than it was.** A diver now has eight states
   and a crew member has one: `Hull` draws crew as plain circles with no state input at all, and
   `crewOverflow` still has no consumer. The more complete the diver half of the picture becomes,
   the more confidently a hull reads "all good" over a divemaster who went back down for a weight
   belt — and **missing crew** is severity 2 in the glossary's unaccounted-for ladder, above every
   clerical kind. Read this item as *the crew marks must carry a roll-call state before any
   recorded tone is reachable*, not merely as *a person with no seat must be drawable*. The geometry caps human beings at `capacity + 2`, and the
   manifest is *every* person on a boat — divers, students, staff, crew, and a support diver with no
   booking at all. A manifest hull must be able to draw a person aboard who has no seat, and
   `crewOverflow` must have a consumer that names the guides the wheelhouse could not hold.

### 4. What no idea changes

`src/lib/readiness.ts`, `src/lib/trip-admission.ts` and every safety rule; the roll call's one tap
per name and the count on the surface; the waiver, the card check, the medical flag; copy from the
bundle in every locale, the clock, the timezone; the storefront's face and the shop's colour; the
booking transaction, Stripe, the integrations, the exports. A redesign that touched a safety rule
would not be a design decision.

## Consequences

- The next twenty design conversations are about one thing, not about rows. A new surface is
  placed on the day, on a boat, or on the chart, and a surface that fits none of them is a question
  worth asking before it is built.
- Round 2's surface lands first and alone (three sessions); the picked idea's home is the first
  visible change (23a) and the storefront's front page the third (23d); the rest follows one family
  per session. Estimate under Tide: five to six sessions after the surface; Deck the same plus one
  schema slice; Chart six to seven plus the coastline decision.
- The pixel baselines change wholesale at 23a and again at each family; each PR explains its diffs
  (the **visual-triage** skill) rather than re-baselining blind.
- `staff-destinations.ts` stops being a nav registry and becomes the idea's registry of places; the
  three consumers that derive from it (`ShopNavLinks`, `StaffTabBar`, the palette) are replaced by
  the idea's own bar and the search, so the invariant "a destination exists in one list or none"
  survives with two consumers instead of three.
- Under Tide, `sun-moon.ts`'s "informs and gates nothing" holds: the sky is a picture of the hour,
  never an input to any rule, and a shop without coordinates gets a neutral sky rather than a wrong
  one.

## Alternatives considered

- **A fourth idea, the person** — the app is the diver in front of you; the counter conversation as
  the home. Rejected as the organising idea: a dive day is not a queue of people, and Clear the
  deck's "by noun" concept already showed where it leads (a list of people over a list of boats).
- **Ask** — the app is a question box. It is the search inside every idea, not an idea about the
  thing.
- **Live Activities on the lock screen** — the day comes to you. The most Apple answer and not
  buildable as a web app; noted, not drawn.
- **Keeping H-87's three directions and adding a fourth** — more choices in a round that already
  had too many; the owner's read was about the premise, not the menu.
- **One idea, drawn alone** — the brief asked for a few options to pick from, and three ideas that
  differ in kind (time, object, place) is the honest small number.
