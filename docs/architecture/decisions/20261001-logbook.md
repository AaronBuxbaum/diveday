# 20261001-logbook — Rebuild the staff frontend on plain nouns, one page shape and the Logbook theme

- **Status:** Accepted 2026-10-01 — **H-91** (Aaron Buxbaum, in session: "do the recommended moves")
- **Date:** 2026-10-01
- **Design:** [DiveDay Redesign Directions](https://claude.ai/artifact/1piVavXUwbFwvLvix4mphn) — the
  evaluation, the keep/merge/cut inventory and four directions with mockups
- **Scope:** every staff surface under `src/app/shop/**`, the staff shell, the destination registry
  `src/lib/staff-destinations.ts`, the design tokens in `src/app/globals.css`, and the theme of the
  diver-facing pages (theme only this round)
- **Supersedes:** [20260919-one-idea](20260919-one-idea.md) (I · Tide: the app as the day, navigation
  by time, the sky band, the departure as a hull with no tabs)

## Context

The owner's read of the shipped Tide build on 2026-10-01: the everyday use case is ugly, because
every screen carries rare and clever controls, and confusing, because the app was stripped too far.
The evaluation measured both. The bar offered three times (Today, Week, Season) and 17 of 21 staff
destinations had no button anywhere; on a phone the whole nav was an unlabeled calendar icon. The
trip page ran to 5,758px at desktop and settings to 68 flat entries. Today was a list of sentences of
about 32 kinds. The look was iOS system grey and blue with a navy sky band, the fifth named system in
a month. Nothing under `src/lib` or `src/db` was at fault.

## Decision

1. **Navigation is nouns you can see.** A sidebar on desktop and a bottom tab bar on phones: Today,
   Schedule, Divers, Inbox, Money, then Courses (shops that teach) and Gear (shops that turn it on),
   Settings at the foot. Phone tabs: Today, Schedule, Divers, Inbox, More. Search stays as a
   shortcut, never the only door, and a diver result opens the diver's record.
2. **One page shape.** Title, at most one primary action, optional tabs, content.
3. **A departure has five tabs**: Divers, Check-in, Boat, Gear, Details, under a stage stepper
   (Prep → Check-in → Aboard → Back). Counter check-in and the manifest move into it. Rare acts (blow
   out, cancel) live behind its ⋯ menu.
4. **Today** is today's departures with stage and readiness, then a short "Needs you" list: a status
   pill and one fix per row, not a sentence.
5. **Theme: Logbook.** Paper `#f4f6f4`, white groups, slate ink `#16232c`, sea teal `#0b6e8a`, a rare
   marine amber `#d97a1e`; a designed dark scheme in deep slate. IBM Plex Sans and Plex Mono via
   `next/font`. Radii 8 (control) and 12 (panel); panels are set off by hairlines, not lift.
6. **Boat mode is Night Dive**: navy `#0a141c`, white ink, marine safety yellow `#ffd23f` as the one
   action colour, Atkinson Hyperlegible, in both schemes. The roll call opens in it and anyone can
   switch it on. It replaces the ambient-light switch, the water lock and the separate glare skin.
7. **The cut list** in the design write-up is approved as marked: the sky band and sun-arc strip,
   the hour-tinted wash, odometer digits and countdown sends, the window sticker, dock sign and boat
   card, the diver's shelf (into `/ready`), the gift giver's page, follow-the-boat, kinds of day and
   seasons, the lobby board and kiosk, the tide/sky/moon lines, and the single-knob settings it
   names. Nothing safety-related is cut.
8. **Diver-facing pages are rethemed only** this round; their redesign follows the staff app.

The build is one stack in the write-up's order: tokens and type, shell, departure, Today, Schedule,
Divers, Inbox and Money merges, Settings, the cuts, the diver retheme. Each layer deletes what it
replaces (H-49).

## Alternatives considered

- **B · Dockside alone** (the boat is the app) — the strongest "wow" and best at the dock, but the
  back office becomes second-class; its stepper is kept on the departure instead.
- **C · Front Desk alone** (a to-do list with a detail pane) — fastest at the counter, weak on a
  phone and wrong on a boat; its list is kept on Today instead.
- **D · Chart** (a week calendar as home) — familiar to switchers but cramped on a phone and the
  least differentiated; it survives as a view inside Schedule.
- **Keep Tide and fix pixels** — the findings are structural; a pixel pass cannot add buttons.

## Consequences

Every visual baseline moves in the first layer, and most staff e2e specs that navigate by the bar
move in the second. A new staffer can find every daily place without search. The registry stops
being a list of times. Revisit if pilot shops cannot find a place from the sidebar or if Boat mode
proves unreadable in direct sun; the tokens are one block in `globals.css`, so a palette change is
cheap, and the nav is one registry with two consumers.
