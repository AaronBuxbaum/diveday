## Context

The owner's brief on 2026-09-08: the app is not consistent in voice, spacing, elegance, delight,
design system and components; look at everything, propose at least three approaches, think
creatively and top-down; the current visual style is not sacred.

Read from the running app with the whole tree counted (three read-only sweeps and screenshots of
twenty surfaces at 1280 and 390), the finding is not a missing design system. The system is good and
mostly adopted: colour drift is at zero, one control class sits on every input, one page header on
40 of 47 staff pages, one empty state, one chrome bar shared by staff and diver pages. The
inconsistency is concentrated in the things a checker cannot see and a page author decides alone:

- **Five page widths** across the staff app (`max-w-2xl` to `6xl`); the content column moves
  848 → 976 → 1104px between three adjacent tabs.
- **The eyebrow says four kinds of thing** — a date on Today, an area on Orders, the title again on
  Settings — in 19 spellings at four sizes; the title beneath it is a noun on five pages, a sentence
  on three, a question on one; seven nav tabs name their page one way and the page names itself
  another.
- **A group carries its count three ways** ("— 1" on Reviews, "· 3" on Inbox, meta on the right on
  Orders), one component, five pages.
- **Five row grammars** plus 31 files that hand-roll a `divide-y` list; the 52px row rung appears
  twice in the tree; one diver renders in eight anatomies across eight pages.
- **"Add a thing" in six spellings**, two of them on the same page.
- **One state drawn five ways**; good news wearing coral's wash, which is the shape of an error;
  a coral progress meter on the counter, where the palette says coral is never a status.
- **27 spellings of one `<summary>`**, 305 spellings of a skeleton bar with no primitive, 57
  spellings of a link with 104 lacking the 44px floor, 7 notice components in 5 shapes, 5 dialog
  implementations.
- **Delight spent along one seam**: every earned moment sits on a page that draws its own layout;
  the twelve terminal outcomes on the six doors that share one shell get a plain sentence.
- **The voice**: 7,390 strings, one rule, a guard that checks five mechanical tells; the clipboard
  fails in seven sentences, "we" is four different actors, "fine" has nine words and "not fine"
  eight, headings run in six grammars, empty states in five shapes, and inside a locale named
  `en-US` colour beats color 15–0 and cancelled beats canceled 30–0.

The cause is procedural, not aesthetic. Five design passes landed between 08-27 and 09-07 —
[Clearwater](20260827-clearwater-surface-language.md), [Reef](20260901-diveday-reimagined.md),
[Reef all the way down](20260904-reef-all-the-way-down.md),
[Before you ask](20260906-before-you-ask.md), [Nothing from nowhere](20260907-nothing-from-nowhere.md)
— and each added a vocabulary (a panel, a dial, a moment, a stage word, a fold) without deleting
the one before. Three grammars now coexist on one screen of Today. A sixth vocabulary would produce
the same feeling in a month.

## Decision

### 1. Six jobs, one spelling each — the floor

Whatever surface the owner picks, these are deleted first and held by a guard. They are
[Direction A](../../design/canvases/20260908-one-hand/DirectionA.dc.html) in full and the first
slice of B, C and D.

| Job | One spelling | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page width | one list width (`max-w-5xl`) and one reading width (`max-w-3xl`), chosen by the page's kind | a guard on `<main>` in `src/app/shop/**` |
| Eyebrow | the destination group from `src/lib/staff-destinations.ts`, always; a date belongs in the lede | `ShopPageHeader` takes the group, not a string |
| Title | the destination's name as the tab says it (H-71 b); the greeting on the home is the one exception | the same registry |
| Section heading | one size wherever it sits; `SECTION_TITLE_CLASS` and `LEAD_TITLE_CLASS` become one | `check:type-ramp` learns the constants |
| Group count | meta on the right, tabular, always | `GroupLabel` drops the free-text count |
| Row | `LedgerRow` with slots; `Table` only where money is a column | a guard on `divide-y` outside `src/components/ui/` |
| Add a thing | the last row of the list it adds to, primary ink, a chevron | a guard on an "Add" control outside that row |
| State | three pills (danger, warning, live) and words; a neutral fact is a sentence, never a grey capsule | `Badge` drops the neutral tone |
| Good news | a check and a sentence; coral is never a box behind reading text and never a meter | one row added to Reef's coral table |
| Meter | the dial for a count that fills; one thin lagoon bar for everything else | `ProgressBar` is the bar |
| Link | the button primitive's link variant | `button.test.ts` refuses the raw string |
| Skeleton | one `Skeleton` primitive on the radius ladder | `src/components/ui/skeleton.tsx` |

With them: `ui/disclosure.tsx` becomes the one `<summary>` spelling (the 27 are swept); the home's
`ActionResultNotice` folds into `FormStatus`; the sixteen loose `?notice=` renders go through
`StaffNoticeBanner`; `TripPageHeader`'s off-ramp title joins the ramp; and two outright bugs are
fixed — `bg-card` on the dive-sites empty state is not a token, and the rental-fit glow hard-codes
an rgba that never flips to the night palette.

### 2. The voice sheet

The same deletion, applied to the bundles, and written once in [brand.md](../../design/brand.md)
so it stops being decided thirty-six times:

- **One actor.** DiveDay, only when it acts; never "we", never "the app", never "the systems".
  The shop's own things are named as the shop's ("Rentals", not "What we rent").
- **One spelling.** American, inside `en-US`; curly apostrophes and quotes; the real ellipsis; the
  en dash for ranges and the em dash nowhere in a label.
- **One separator.** The middle dot joins facts; a group's count is meta, never joined to its label.
- **Titles are nouns.** The tab's word (H-71 b). A sentence in the title slot is a lede that took
  the heading's place.
- **One word for fine, one for not.** The readiness resolver's pair, Ready and Blocked, on every
  row; Connected for a third party; Needs service for a unit; nothing else.
- **One sentence per event.** The clipboard fails in one sentence across ten keys; the same for
  saved, sent, copied. Duplicated strings move to `shared.json`.
- **Every description under 120 characters or deleted**, by the copy-restraint filter; a mechanism
  ("retry queues", "reconciliation ledgers", "S3 API origin", "encryption key", "instance",
  "README") never reaches a shop owner.
- **No arrows, exclamation marks or emoji in a bundle.** The 26 "→", 13 "!" and 7 emoji go.

`check:voice` grows from its five rules to the sheet's mechanical half (the glyphs, the arrows, the
banned words, the two spellings, the count-in-label). The register half stays judgement, run once
across the tree by slice 20e rather than per feature.

### 3. The surface is the owner's (H-71 a) — round 2

**Round 1 (2026-09-08)** drew four directions: A (decision 1 alone), B the logbook, C the
instrument, D the briefing. **The owner's read (2026-09-09):** A is the best of the four but wants
more "wow"; C is hard to move around in and not warm enough; B is not efficient and not what a dive
shop wants to deal with; D feels like too many existing apps; and all four felt too close to ideas
already tried. Read against the earlier canvases that is right — C was Deck (09-01) and B was Tide —
so B, C and D are declined and stay on the canvas as the record.

**Round 2 (2026-09-09)** keeps A as the base and adds five levers, one per board, none drawn by any
earlier canvas. Each is A plus one thing, never a second spelling of anything decision 1 holds at
one:

- **E · Rooms** — the shop drawn as its own places: Today is the board behind the counter (a column
  per boat, name cards, crew magnets, the desk as a tray), Gear is the wall (tags on hooks), Check-in
  the counter, Divers the drawer. One card at three sizes on Reef's tokens; no textures. A second
  grammar beside the ledger, so it wins as the whole staff app or joins the lobby display only.
- **F · The tide line** — one 64px strip under the chrome on every staff page: the day as a line,
  departures as blocks that fill as divers board, a moving "now", the next boat lit. The page beneath
  is A. Reef's hourly water band becomes the line's wash. Renders nothing on a day with no boats.
- **H · Their sea** — Harbor comes inside: the staff app takes the shop's colour for its lagoon
  through Harbor's existing derivation, and each departure's header carries a site photograph (the
  shop's own, then the catalogue's, then the hand's mark). No photograph on a manifest, roll call,
  waiver, payment or the counter; signal colours and coral do not move.
- **I · The one page** — no tabs: Now, Later, the desk, the shop as one scroll with a rail that says
  where you are and search as the door to any record; a register opens as a sheet over the page.
- **K · Together** — presence: who is in the shop and where, one chip on the row a colleague is
  acting on, a one-tap hand-off on a Need, and rows that settle live when someone else settles them.
  Needs a live layer; presence shows place and act, never a screen, and keeps no history; the roll
  call stays commit-then-render.

The recommendation, stated so it can be disagreed with: **A, then F and H together, then K.** F and
H are cheap, compose with anything, and between them answer both halves of the brief — the tide line
is the efficiency and the "not another list" in one strip, the shop's sea is the warmth. K is the
biggest wow and the biggest build. E is the most striking single board and the right shape for the
lobby display whatever else is picked. I is the honest alternative for a phone-first shop and the
one that changes the most.

#### Round 1, for the record

Four directions are drawn. A is decision 1 with nothing else. B, C and D each replace the surface
and keep decision 1 underneath:

- **B · The logbook** — every surface a page of the shop's one book: a 112px margin column, ruled
  entries, a figure or a verb in a 200px right column, a stamp when a thing is settled. Newsreader
  for names and titles, Geist for sentences, Geist Mono in the margin. No panels, shadows, pills or
  chevrons. Needs H-71 d.
- **C · The instrument** — the staff tool as a dive computer: dark by default, one cyan for every
  action, every number a figure in Geist Mono, every state a word in a fixed 128px slot. Two
  components, a readout and a line.
- **D · The briefing** — the voice as the system: five sentence shapes (State, Need, Fact, Offer,
  Done), each with a fixed order of who · what · one verb, and the controls as verbs inside the
  sentences. The kind word, the chevron and the trailing action go. Reef's palette and the hand
  stay.

The recommendation, stated so it can be disagreed with: **A now, then D.** The floor is two
sessions of deletion and the guards that keep it deleted. D is the one surface change whose
consistency a script can read, it keeps the palette and the hand the owner chose on 09-01, and it
is the one a shop owner would describe to another in a sentence. B is the most distinctive and the
most expensive; C is the best on a boat and the worst at a desk.

### 5. Every surface, one hand — round 3

**The owner's read of round 2 (2026-09-09):** "I like everything you've done here and I want to
keep pushing these concepts farther. Let's keep cleaning up, iterating, and forming innovative
concepts that will make customers go 'wow'. Make sure we're designing every surface of the app."

Round 3 answers in three parts on the canvas's third page.

- **The kit.** A with F, H and K composed into one sheet — tokens with one input (the shop's
  colour, through Harbor's derivation; DiveDay's lagoon on its own pages), nine type sizes, one page
  anatomy (chrome, the tide line, one 976px column or a 720px reading column, the eyebrow as the
  group, the title as the tab's word, the count as meta, one row, the add row last), three pills and
  the words, one notice, one disclosure, one skeleton, one dial and one bar, the panel with its photo
  band and the caption that names whose picture it is, presence, night and boat as the same
  components on other tokens. Every round-3 board is drawn from it and nothing else; a board that
  needs more says so under "New here" and the sheet gains it or the board loses it.
- **Every surface.** All eighty-one page routes, on fifteen surface boards, each board naming what
  today's page loses ("Deleted here") and what it gains ("New here"). The audit's six counts are zero
  on the page by construction; slices 20a–20e make them zero in the tree, and slice 20m applies each
  board's deletion list to its family of pages.
- **Eight more levers**, each one thing on top of the kit that no earlier canvas drew:
  - **L · One line, two sides** — the trip's own line (check-in, boards, leaves, the sites, the
    surface interval, back) drawn once and shown on the staff trip page, the diver's trip page and
    thread, the lobby board and the reminder email; now moves on all of them from the crew's stage
    taps, and says "as of" when the last tap is stale.
  - **M · The pass** — booking confirmed becomes a pass in the shop's colour with the site's photo,
    the boat, the slip, the time, what to bring and a code carrying the booking id only; the counter
    scans it and the diver's screen says "You're aboard" in the second the desk's row settles.
    Composes the Wallet slice (18f). A scan is a convenience, never a gate.
  - **N · The postcard** — the recap as a postcard printed from the crew's log: front the site and
    the date, back the dives, the crew, what you saw, the review as one line and "next time" with a
    reason. Never for a cancelled trip, a no-show, or an unsent log.
  - **O · The log** — Reports becomes the shop's log: the close-out writes the day's entry from a
    sentence DiveDay proposes, the season is a strip of days, the month's figures come from the
    entries; the title stays the tab's word.
  - **P · The room in the empty state** — round 2's rooms live on as six line drawings inside the
    empty states and on the lobby display, never as a second grammar for a list. This answers E.
  - **Q · Ask the desk** — ⌘K answers in the row grammar (shipped in part on 09-06); a unit and a
    day's line join the answers.
  - **R · The shop's card** — Settings opens on the shop's card, and brand edits repaint it live.
  - **S · First light** — a new shop's first Today is the empty tide line with one invitation; the
    wizard that would follow the onboard door is deleted before it is built.

The recommendation, stated so it can be disagreed with: **the composed base on every page (a); then
L, M and O; then N; then P, R and S together; Q as it ships.** L is nearly free after F and lands on
both sides; M and O are the two a diver and an owner would each describe to a friend.

**The owner's round-3 calls (H-71):** (a) the composed base as the surface on every page — the ADR
moves to Accepted on it; (e) which of L–S ride, in what order; (f) whether the pass carries a code
and the counter may scan it; (g) whether the close-out writes an editable entry and Reports keeps its
title; (h) whether the line moves on the crew's stage taps with "as of", or on the roll call's
checkpoints only. The round-2 sub-calls (H's colour and photographs, K's presence by name, E's board)
stand; every round-3 board assumes yes to H and K, and P answers E.

### 6. Twelve angles, seven possibilities — round 4

**The owner's read of round 3 (2026-09-10):** "Let's continue to creatively evaluate the entire app
for opportunities for improvement. Think about the app from many different angles and show me a few
interesting possibilities we can do. Remember we want this to be elegant and make potential
customers go 'wow' when they see it, both in marketing and in-app."

Rounds 1 to 3 read the app by surface. Round 4 reads it by angle on the canvas's fourth page:
time (the day, the year), the people around the diver, the sea and the sky, money given, the
physical world, the first minute, the diver's memory, trust and safety, the crew, silence, and
DiveDay's own pages. Each angle names what the app does today and where it stops; the day, trust
and silence are answered by earlier levers or by the bans and say so; seven gaps become
possibilities, each one thing on top of the kit, in round 3's fiction:

- **T · The shop's year** — the log (O) rolls up into a year page above Reports' month: the strip of
  days, the four figures, the sites by times dived, the entries; the same page prints as a card in
  the shop's face (divers, boats and sites, never money), and with one switch becomes the proof
  band on DiveDay's homepage.
- **U · Follow the boat** — the pass carries "Share with whoever is waiting for you"; the link opens
  the trip's line (L) in the shop's face with the stage word the crew tapped and its time, and
  nothing else: no names, no counts of people, no position. The storefront's Right now and the dock
  sign carry the same line; a Settings switch, off by default, turns all of it off.
- **V · The living reef** — the crew's log (N) gains Seen from the shop's species list; the site
  page shows "Seen here this month" with dates and how often, the storefront's site cards say it in
  one sentence, and the tide line carries sunrise, sunset and the moon as ticks. "Seen" is a record
  with a date, never a promise.
- **W · Give a dive** — a seat bought for someone else arrives as a pass (M) with the giver's line
  and a claim link; the receiver claims it and signs their own waiver; a blow-out refunds the giver.
  A gift is a booking, not stored value, so gift cards stay parked. The postcard's back gains "Bring
  a buddy next time", which is the roadmap's counted share-the-boat door.
- **X · The shop on paper** — Settings gains Print: the dock sign, the boat card by day and by night
  (in the boat's colours, never the shop's), the window sticker, the paper pass, the site briefing
  card, the year poster; every sheet reads what its page reads and carries the day it was printed.
  The print sheet joins the kit as a specimen.
- **Y · Try it with your boats** — the homepage's hero takes three fields (the shop, a boat, the
  first departure) and redraws as the visitor's first Today (S) in a colour derived from their
  name, with the onboard door already filled and their storefront in miniature beneath. DiveDay
  looks nothing up and stores nothing until the door is opened.
- **Z · The diver's shelf** — the capability link is promoted from one trip to the diver at the
  shop: one page with the next seat in a tap, every dive as a postcard, the file and whether it is
  verified, the sizes, the buddy, and "Forget this phone". The storefront greets a known phone by
  name. No medical answer ever appears on it; erasure (H-02) removes it with the record.

The marketing thread that runs through them: DiveDay's own pages carry nothing that is not, or
could not be, a screenshot — a real shop's year card, a real boat's line, a reef page the crew
wrote, paper the shop printed — each with that shop's yes.

The recommendation, stated so it can be disagreed with: **U and Y first (a session each, both on
the homepage); then T; then W and Z; then V and X.**

**The owner's round-4 calls (H-71):** (i) which of T–Z ride, in what order; (j) whether a boat's
line may be public by link and on the storefront, stage word and time only, off by default; (k)
whether DiveDay's pages may show a real shop's year card and boat line with that shop's yes; (l)
whether a seat may be bought for someone else as a pass with a claim link, and whether the counted
buddy seat rides on the postcard; (m) whether the capability link is promoted to the diver at the
shop, with "Forget this phone" and no medical answer on it; (n) whether the crew's log may carry
sightings and the site page show them dated, and whether the tide line may carry sun and moon; (o)
whether Settings gains a print register and the kit a print sheet. Every round-4 board assumes yes
to (a) and to L, M, N and O, because T–Z stand on them.

### 4. What does not change

The name and the mark. Harbor — the diver-facing pages wear the shop's brand, and no direction
touches whose colours the storefront wears. The dock test. The coral bans on manifests, roll call,
certs, waivers and payments. The claims policy. Boat mode, Geist and high-contrast under every
direction. Every settled question in [settled-questions.md](../../design/settled-questions.md).

## Alternatives considered

- **A sixth vocabulary.** Another canvas that adds a look without deleting the previous three.
  Declined: it is what the last five did, and the audit is the result.
- **A component-library rewrite** (a third-party kit under the tokens). Declined: the primitives
  are not the problem — adoption of the ones that exist is at 90% or better wherever a guard holds
  it, and at 10–30% wherever one does not. The work is guards and deletion, not new components.
- **Freeze the design and stop.** Declined by the brief: the owner asked for cohesion and said the
  style is not sacred. But the freeze is half-adopted here: no new vocabulary lands until decision 1
  is complete.
- **Pick the surface for the owner.** Declined: the last three canvases each held the pick open and
  the owner made it in session within a day. Taste is the owner's; the floor is not.

## Consequences

- Two to three sessions of deletion before any pixel is new; the app looks like today, tidier,
  until H-71 a is answered.
- Four new guards (`<main>` width, `divide-y`, the add row, the link string) and two grown ones
  (`check:type-ramp`, `check:voice`), each with a baseline that ratchets down.
- Seven nav tabs and their pages agree on a name; three page titles that were sentences become
  nouns, and their sentences move to the lede.
- The bundles lose roughly forty duplicated keys and gain a shared set; the Spanish bundle is swept
  in the same slice.
- If B is picked, a second face enters the staff app, reversing the Geist-only pick of H-64; if C,
  the staff app is dark by default and Reef's three moments are re-cut; if D, the body ramp steps
  up to 17 and 20 and the desktop column narrows to 720 on reading surfaces. (Moot since the round-1
  read; kept for the record.)
- Round 3 adds no vocabulary and three tables: a pass code on a booking (an id, nothing else), a
  day's log entry, and a crew's dive log per departure; one image render for the postcard; a scan on
  the counter using the device's camera; and one stylesheet block every round-3 board carries
  verbatim, which the canvas README says how to change.
- The diver-facing surfaces enter this ADR's scope; Harbor's derivation and the coral and photo
  bans do not move, and the kit's diver frames are drawn in the shop's face and colour as shipped.
- Round 4 adds no vocabulary beyond one print specimen, and four small things in the tree if its
  calls are yes: two columns on the crew's dive log (species, count), a giver and a claim link on a
  booking (the party's shape), a token scope of diver-at-shop beside the booking scope, and three
  shop switches (the public boat, the year on DiveDay's pages, the lobby display); one public route
  per boat per day that carries a stage word and a time only; a sun-and-moon function in `src/lib/`
  with no dependency; and DiveDay's own pages reading the shops that said yes. U, W and Z get a
  security review before merge; X's boat card gets the dive-domain review.
