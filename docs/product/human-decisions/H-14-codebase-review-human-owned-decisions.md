# H-14: The codebase review's "Human-owned decisions" item 1: current ADRs let every staff role reach…

- **Status:** Implemented
- **Human owner:** Product owner

## Decision or approval needed

The [codebase review](../../architecture/decisions/20260724-staff-session-and-capability-migration-policy.md#context)'s "Human-owned decisions" item 1: current ADRs let every staff role reach nearly every staff surface, while product language implies owners/managers configure money and crew/captains run operations. Does DiveDay need real role boundaries on payment settings, refunds, waiver templates, diver deletion, and trip configuration?

## Minimum outcome to record

**Decided 2026-07-24: yes**, and **implemented 2026-07-24**. Five predicates in `src/lib/authz.ts` (`canManagePaymentSettings`, `canRefund`, `canManageWaiverTemplates`, `canDeleteDiver` → owner/manager; `canConfigureTrips` → owner/manager/instructor), with live DB-checked companions in `src/db/authz.ts` (`loadActiveStaffRoles` + `canPersonX`) so a demoted/disabled/deleted staff member loses the surface immediately (closing the H-15 window for money, legal text, and roster deletion). Enforced in both layers per ADR-0006 — each surface's page hides the control **and** its server action(s)/route re-check, refunds gated in `removeBookingAction`/`refundPaymentAction`/order `refundAction`, trip config across `trips/[id]` and `trips/new`. See [20260724-role-authorization](../../architecture/decisions/20260724-role-authorization.md). **Amended 2026-08-20 (gear):** deleting a gear unit stays **any-staff**, deliberately. The register is ungated by H-06 (substituting and handing over gear is day work), and the history-preserving exit a shop actually wants is `retired`, which every role can already reach; the only thing delete removes is the service log of a unit somebody mistyped minutes ago, and sending a deckhand to a manager for their own typo costs more than it protects. Revisit once a pilot shop holds real service history — the H-15 window is accepted until then. **Amended 2026-09-10 (inbox):** reading the shop inbox and answering a diver are **any-staff**, both of them. The inbox shipped on 2026-09-07 behind `canAnswerShopInbox` (owner/manager) on two arguments — a reply leaves as the shop, and the list carries contact details for people who never booked — and the owner read both the other way (issues #1505/#1518): the message that most wants answering at 7am is answered by whoever is at the dock, and the insider risk of a staffer typing over the shop's own sender is the one a shop manages by choosing who it employs. Both predicates are deleted rather than relaxed, so the surface and the action carry what every ungated staff surface carries — the live-roles check in `requireShopSurface` / `requireDiverActionContext`, which still drops a demoted, disabled or deleted account on its next request. Today's `unanswered_messages` row widened with it. See [20260907-two-way-inbox](../../architecture/decisions/20260907-two-way-inbox.md) decision 9. **Amended 2026-09-16 (Aaron Buxbaum, ready-for-human decision deck), issue #1679: Date requests is opened the same way.** It was owner/manager on the written ground that the rows carry contact details for people who have not booked — which is exactly what the inbox now shows every live staff role, for a message from an address with no diver record, and lets them answer in the shop's name. A rule that refuses a captain on one tab and admits them on the next is not a line, it is two features' shipping dates. The commercial reason (deciding which unscheduled day is worth a boat is the desk's work) lost on the argument that opened the inbox: whoever is at the counter on Tuesday is best placed to answer a diver asking for a Tuesday. Every use of `canPersonViewShopReports` **over date-request rows** is deleted rather than relaxed — on the page, and at the four downstream sites that read the same rows for the schedule builder and the two booking doors, where the check was justified in comments as "the same live report gate that protects /requests" and would otherwise have left the surface open with its only two acts silently doing nothing (`security-reviewer`, 2026-09-16). The predicate itself survives untouched everywhere revenue actually renders. The refusal notice and both locales' sentence go with the gate, and `/calls` — ungated, and redirecting its date-request outcome straight at this page — stops sending a captain into a refusal for a row they had just written.
