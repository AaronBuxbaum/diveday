# H-09: Which channel sends booking and waiver notifications, and what consent, copy, timing, and sender…

- **Status:** In progress
- **Human owner:** Product owner + communications owner

## Decision or approval needed

Which channel sends booking and waiver notifications, and what consent, copy, timing, and sender identity apply?

## Minimum outcome to record

Provider: AWS SES (switched from Resend 2026-08-03, [20260803-ses-sole-email-provider](../../architecture/decisions/20260803-ses-sole-email-provider.md); AWS-side production access/DKIM/credentials still outstanding). The implemented baseline is immediate transactional booking confirmation, staff-triggered waiver link, and — new — the wait-list freed-seat invite, all through the one `notify()` seam; `SES_FROM_EMAIL` is the verified sender. Each of these sends for real when SES is configured and degrades to a copyable/mailto composer or a hand-off link when it isn't. The owner dashboard shows the latest failed or unconfigured send. **The remaining channel and cadence scope is now implemented:** courtesy SMS via an AWS SNS `notifySms()` seam ([20260802-sns-sms-adapter](../../architecture/decisions/20260802-sns-sms-adapter.md), superseding the original Twilio-backed design) and scheduled pre-trip reminder cadences (7-day and 24-hour) sent by an idempotent cron endpoint ([20260721-scheduled-reminder-cadence](../../architecture/decisions/20260721-scheduled-reminder-cadence.md)), both degrading to `not_configured` until their provider env is set. Still open (policy, not mechanism): opt-in/consent policy, sender ownership, approved copy, the `CRON_SECRET` and `SNS_AWS_*` credential owners, and per-channel delivery monitoring/retries. **Policy decided 2026-07-24 (Aaron Buxbaum):** sender identity is the **shop's own name/brand** (already how every template greets — `src/lib/notifications/email.ts`); the existing transactional templates (booking confirmation, waiver link, freed-seat invite, 7-day/24-hour reminders) are **approved as the launch copy** — no new template library needed, the H-09 mechanism already shipped them. **Email is transactional/service messaging** (booking, waiver, trip logistics), so no marketing opt-in is required to send it. Credential owners for `SES_FROM_EMAIL`, `CRON_SECRET`, and `SNS_AWS_*` are **Aaron Buxbaum** (sole operator; same as H-04). Launch-minimum delivery monitoring is the existing owner-dashboard "latest failed/unconfigured send"; per-channel retries stay a later enhancement. **Still gated on the attorney: SMS opt-in consent** (TCPA and equivalents) — excluded from this decision; keep the SNS channel behind its unset env until that clears. **Extended 2026-08-14 (Aaron Buxbaum): the transactional basis above covers messages attached to a booking that exists, and nothing else.** Abandoned-checkout recovery is therefore **commercial** — its recipient started a checkout and never finished one, and may have abandoned it because they changed their mind about the shop — so it now carries an unsubscribe link and honours the courtesy-email opt-out like the wait-list invite and last-minute deal beside it. Consent is resolved through the checkout's own address (a party checkout has no lead booking to key on); where that address answers to nobody in the shop, the mail is not sent rather than sent without a way out of it. This is the rule for the next borderline send, not only for this one. See [20260814-checkout-recovery-is-commercial](../../architecture/decisions/20260814-checkout-recovery-is-commercial.md). **Settled 2026-08-14 (Aaron Buxbaum): the not-sent outcome is the permanent answer, not a gap** (FU-20260814-checkout-recovery-cannot-reach-a-non-diver-purchaser, closed unbuilt). DiveDay does **not** hold an email address with no person behind it, including for the narrow purpose of remembering that somebody asked to be left alone. A shop-scoped suppression list would serve one real case -- a parent paying for their children's seats with an address that is on no diver's record -- but it buys that by storing a non-customer's address indefinitely, which is a privacy cost as well as a privacy benefit, and both current callers of `startBookingCheckout` pass an address belonging to a person. So `checkout-recovery.ts`'s `unaddressable` counter is intended behaviour: the recovery nudge only ever reaches somebody the shop already has a record for. Revisit if DiveDay ever lets one person pay for a party they are not in.
