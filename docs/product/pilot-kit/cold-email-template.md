# Cold email to a dive shop — template

The letter that opens a conversation with a shop the founder has no warm route into. It is the
written cousin of the [first-call script](first-call-script.md), and it inherits that script's
posture: **it is not a pitch.** Its job is to start a relationship with one person — which in
practice means a one-line reply, or a forward to whoever runs the boat.

The founder personalizes and sends every one of these by hand, from his own mailbox. Nothing here
is sent automatically, in a sequence, or in bulk (`commercial-outreach` skill: no autonomous
outreach). Governed by the [claims policy](../marketing.md#claims-policy-hard-rules) exactly as a
public page is.

## The problem this email is written against

The reader is not shopping. Whatever they use today works well enough, the season is on, and they
delete software email without reading past line one. **Switching cost is the blocker, not price**
([first-call script](first-call-script.md), hypothesis 5) — so a free offer does not solve the
objection, and leading with one signals that we misread the room.

The deeper trap is subtler: **anything shaped like sales is answered like sales.** Objection
handling, risk reversal, a benefits list, a discount — each one tells the reader this is a
transaction, and a transaction with a stranger who has no customers is an easy no. The email has to
be a letter from a person who wants to learn something, because that is what it actually is.

Four consequences shape every line below:

1. **Nothing is asked that requires a change.** No trial, no signup, no demo homework, no
   "15 minutes to show you". The reader can satisfy this email with four words typed at a dock.
2. **It asks more than it tells.** One sentence says what DiveDay is. Nothing else describes the
   product, because a description invites evaluation and evaluation of an unproven product ends
   one way.
3. **No offer.** See [Why the offer is not in this email](#why-the-offer-is-not-in-this-email).
4. **The honest weakness is the hook, not a confession to bury.** The founder has never run a shop,
   and the offline count has never been on a boat. That is the truthful reason to be writing to
   shops one at a time rather than advertising, and it is the one opener no marketing department
   would approve — which is why it gets read.

**Scope: this template is written for the `boat` profile** on the
[call list](florida-call-list.md) — its load-bearing paragraph is the count between dives. A
course-heavy shop or a defector needs a different opener; do not retro-fit the boat paragraph onto
one, because improvising it at send time is exactly where template-smell gets in.

## Before sending — the research, ten minutes per shop

`[RESEARCHED DETAIL]` is the load-bearing slot. Fill it with **one thing read on the shop's own
pages**: a named departure and time, a specific trip on their schedule, the charter they run
Saturdays. It must be current and checkable.

- **If nothing can be verified, use the no-detail version** of the question below. Sending with no
  detail beats sending with a guess: a wrong detail proves the email is a template and confirms the
  reader's no.
- **Never guess what software the shop uses, and never name a vendor.** It cannot be verified from
  outside, and being wrong ends the conversation.
- Look up the owner's or manager's first name. If the only address is a general inbox (`info@`,
  `dive@`, a contact form), send the [forwarding variant](#forwarding-variant--a-general-inbox)
  instead.

Never invent a shop to send to — the [call list](florida-call-list.md)'s rule binds here too.

## The email

**Subject:** `the hour before the boat leaves`

Alternates: `never been on a real boat` · `a question about your Saturday mornings`

**No merge field in the subject line.** `Saturday mornings at [SHOP NAME]` is the most recognizable
sales-tool shape there is, and it is spotted before the email is opened.

> [FIRST NAME],
>
> I write software and I dive, and for a while now I've been building something for running dive
> days, booking through the head count on the boat. I've never run a shop. That's why this is a
> question rather than a pitch.
>
> Most of what I know came from one owner walking me through an ordinary Saturday. Whiteboard,
> clipboard, a spreadsheet someone updates by hand. He said the worst hour was the one before the
> boat left. I've been building off that single conversation longer than I should have.
>
> The part I can't work out from a desk is the water. The count between dives, three miles out,
> where the bars run out. Divers get counted there with the radio off, but it has never been on a
> real boat, and I'm not willing to say it holds until someone has had it in the sun with wet hands.
>
> So: is [RESEARCHED DETAIL] anything like that morning, or is that hour already sorted and I'm
> describing a problem you don't have? Either answer is useful to me.
>
> If the boat side is someone else's call there, I'd be glad to be pointed their way.
>
> Aaron Buxbaum
> [CELL]
> dive.day

Slots: `[FIRST NAME]`, `[RESEARCHED DETAIL]`, `[CELL]`. Nothing else is variable.

**If nothing about the shop could be verified**, the question paragraph is sent in this form
instead — written out because the alternative is splicing a fragment at 11pm on the eleventh email
of the week, which is where template-smell gets in:

> So: does that hour exist at your place, or do your trip mornings run themselves and I'm
> describing a problem you don't have? Either answer is useful to me.

### Before you hit send

Six checks, in the order they'd fail:

- [ ] **From name is a person.** "Aaron Buxbaum", never "DiveDay" or "Aaron at DiveDay". Every
      mobile client renders the sender above the subject, so it is the first thing read and the
      cheapest tell to get wrong.
- [ ] **Plain text, from a real mailbox.** No HTML, no logo, no tracking pixel, one link. A
      marketing email is identified by its formatting before a word of it is read, and a template
      that announces itself defeats every sentence above.
- [ ] **No merge field in the subject line.**
- [ ] **`[RESEARCHED DETAIL]` is something actually read on their pages** — or the no-detail
      version above is used instead. No guesses, and no vendor named.
- [ ] **A real cell number under the signature.** It is the mechanism for the phone call this email
      is hoping for: a mildly curious owner will text a number at 9pm, and will not click, book, or
      schedule.
- [ ] **Send date written into the call list's `Last contact` column**, with the follow-up date in
      `Next action`. "About six business days later" is only enforceable if it is written down
      somewhere ([florida-call-list.md](florida-call-list.md)).

### Why each part is there

| Part | Doing what |
| --- | --- |
| "I've never run a shop" | The first paragraph's real content. It sets the relationship — someone asking, not someone selling — and it is why the rest of the email is allowed to ask a question. One clause says what DiveDay is, and the paragraph does not return to it. |
| The Saturday paragraph | Describes a shop rather than a product, and attributes it to a named third party the founder actually spoke to. It asserts nothing about *this* shop, so there is nothing to defend against. "Longer than I should have" is the admission that makes the question sincere rather than rhetorical. |
| The water paragraph | The genuine reason this email exists rather than an ad, and the thing the reader has that the founder needs. It asserts only the half that is true and says the rest is untested — see [the offline line](#the-offline-line--read-this-before-editing-the-water-paragraph). |
| The question, last | Asked up front it reads as qualification; asked after the reader knows why, answering feels like helping. "Either answer is useful to me" is what makes a *no* safe to send — without it the reader assumes a no invites a rebuttal and says nothing at all. |
| "I'd be glad to be pointed their way" | The forwarding hatch, phrased as a request to a person rather than an instruction to a lead. A reader who isn't the decider gets a way to not-decide, which is the outcome this email most often wins. |
| What is **absent** | No benefits list, no "nothing gets switched off", no export pitch, no scope disclaimer, no offer. Every one of those is objection handling, and objection handling is what tells a reader they are being sold to. They belong in the reply, where they answer a question that was actually asked. |

### The offline line — read this before editing the water paragraph

"Divers get counted there with the radio off, but it has never been on a real boat" is doing two
jobs, and each half is load-bearing.

**The assertion is scoped to the half that is true.** Divers can be counted on a device copy with
no signal; **the crew half cannot** — `src/components/OfflineManifestView.tsx` says it plainly:
*"Divers can be counted with the radio off; crew cannot, in this slice."* A checkpoint needs both
halves, so an after-dive count offshore stays **open** on a device copy exactly as it would online.
The email therefore says *divers get counted*, never *roll call works offline* or *after-dive roll
calls work from that copy*. Widening it to the second phrasing makes it wrong about the one
checkpoint where a person may still be in the water.

**The disclosure is the hook, not a hedge.** Recruiting this reader **is** the documented path to
V-02 ([rollout.md](../rollout.md#03-field-validation-v-01-v-02-v-04-rehearsal)): the field test is
meant to run on "a friendly local charter or the first design-partner candidate", and the
[run sheet](v-02-field-test-run-sheet.md) is the checklist for the morning it happens. Saying it
has never been on a boat is what gives a stranger a reason this is not an ad.

Two edits to refuse: widening the assertion past the diver half, and deleting "it has never been on
a real boat" once V-02 is recorded without replacing the paragraph's reason for existing. The offer
to the reader is *help me find out*; if that stops being true the email needs a different third
paragraph, not a stronger adjective.

## Why the offer is not in this email

Six free months is authorized (H-12, amended 2026-08-12) and it is deliberately **not** stated here.

A discount is the most advertising-shaped thing an email can contain. Arriving unasked, in the same
paragraph as "nobody uses this yet", it invites the reader to price the risk of being first — which
is arithmetic that never comes out in our favour and which the email never needed them to do. It
also manufactures the question *what's the catch* at the exact moment the letter should be closing,
and it converts a discovery conversation into a price negotiation before the founder has learned
anything.

The sibling artifact already settled this: [first-call script](first-call-script.md) §E is titled
"**Only if they ask:** the offer." An email that volunteers on contact what the call withholds
until asked is two artifacts in one kit pointing opposite ways.

So the offer lives in the reply and on the call, stated in
[the one-pager's exact terms](../stakeholders/design-partner-one-pager.md) plus the six-month
amendment. **Do not** re-add it to the first send. If a shop asks what it costs — a good reply, and
a common one — that is the moment, and the answer is a link to the pricing page and the six months
in one sentence.

## Follow-up — about six business days later

Same thread, no new subject, no guilt, and an easy out. It **re-asks the question** rather than only
offering a door marked no: six days on, in a threaded reply with the original collapsed, a message
whose only frictionless action is a decline harvests closure instead of conversations, and nothing
at all from the many readers who simply forgot.

> One follow-up, then I'll stop.
>
> Still the same question: is the hour before the boat leaves a problem where you are, or is it
> already sorted? Either answer is useful to me. If it's a no, or just not this season, say the
> word and I'll leave it there.
>
> Aaron

## Forwarding variant — a general inbox

For `info@`, `dive@`, or a contact form, where the reader is certainly not the decider and the
entire goal is that they pass it on.

> **Subject:** `for whoever runs the boat schedule`
>
> I'm writing about the hour before a boat leaves. Waivers, cert cards, knowing who's actually
> aboard. I write software, I've never run a shop, and I'm trying to learn from people who have,
> which is why I'm writing to shops one at a time instead of advertising. If that's someone's job
> there, would you pass this along? If it isn't, no reply needed.
>
> Aaron Buxbaum
> [CELL]
> dive.day

The subject is a routing instruction, which is the right shape for a gatekeeper. The body must not
open on a category sentence ("I built software for running a dive day") — a front-desk person
triaging `info@` sorts by *does this look like a vendor*, and a category sentence looks like a
vendor in its first eight words. Open on the hour, the same as the main email.

## Cadence, and what counts as working

**Ten to fifteen a week, sent one at a time, by hand.** Not a sequencer, not a mail merge.

**A good reply is four words from a phone at the dock.** "We're fine, thanks" is a good reply — it
is a live thread and a person who now knows the founder's name. So are "what's it cost", "send me
the link", and "that's Dave's, copying him". A forward is the best outcome available and the last
line is built for it. Do not measure this in calls booked.

Every reply gets a call note within the hour, in the
[first-call script](first-call-script.md#within-ten-minutes-of-hanging-up)'s format. A reply that
changes a standing assumption is H-31 evidence and belongs in
[human-decisions.md](../human-decisions.md), never here.

**The 25-shop cap counts shops that take the offer, not emails sent.** H-12's founding cohort is
capped at 25, and six free months is a promise kept by hand with no product mechanism behind it
(see the flags below) — so the number to watch is acceptances. It binds the day a twenty-fifth shop
says yes, which is the day to stop stating the term, not the day to stop writing.

## If they write back — the improvise-a-commitment trap

The temptation on a warm reply is to say yes to whatever is asked. Each of these is off limits, and
each is an existing rule rather than a new one:

- **A ship date, or "I could build that for you."** One developer, no customers; a promise made in
  a reply becomes the thing the shop judges everything by.
- **Anything about how fast support answers.** That promise was retired 2026-08-05
  ([H-12/H-26](../human-decisions.md#decision-register)) and has not been replaced.
- **A discount, a longer free term, or a price different from the site's.** Six months is the whole
  commercial position. Anything beyond it gets written down and taken away, never agreed to live.
- **The price as a number in prose.** Send the pricing page; the figure has one source
  (`earlyAccessPrice` in `src/lib/marketing.ts`).
- **Roll call working without signal, on the boat, or with no bars.** The crew half of a checkpoint
  is **read-only** on a device copy today, so an after-dive count offshore cannot be closed from it
  unless the crew results were already in the snapshot. That is the finding V-02 exists to produce;
  do not describe it as solved.
- **A field test that stands in for the shop's own paper.** V-02 rides *alongside* the printed
  manifest and whatever the crew already does, never in place of either; a missing, expired, or
  stale device copy is never a boarding source (H-05, [run sheet](v-02-field-test-run-sheet.md)).
  If a shop offers "just run it on Saturday's charter", the answer is yes *and* paper, never yes
  instead of paper. The stop rule gets said out loud to the crew on the morning, not written into
  an email.
- **Anything implying DiveDay checks a card with an agency.** There is no agency integration: a
  staffer looks the number up in the agency's own portal and marks it certified. DiveDay holds the
  evidence and the gate; a human does the verifying.
- **That any of it satisfies the Coast Guard, a regulator, or an insurer.** DiveDay records what a
  crew did and prints it. Whether that satisfies anyone is their auditor's call, and no version of
  "compliant" is authorized.
- **Offering to log into their current system or "just get the data out for you."** They export,
  the founder maps. That is the
  [portability-wedge legal guardrail](../assessments/competitive-strategy.md#the-portability-wedge),
  not a preference.
- **"Another shop told me…" or "shops usually…"** There are no other shops.

Not knowing something is a fine answer with this audience. An invented one costs the account.

## Flagged — settle before the offer is stated on a call

- **Six free months is a new commercial term.** What was on the books is *free through the pilot*
  for design partners ([rollout.md](../rollout.md#the-offer-write-it-down-say-it-the-same-way-every-time))
  and a three-week self-serve trial. Recorded as an H-12 amendment on 2026-08-12; the legal/tax
  dependency H-12 carries for binding commercial commitments is **still open**.
- **The product contradicts the offer on day 22.** `TRIAL_DURATION_DAYS = 21` in `src/lib/trial.ts`,
  and the shop's own Settings then renders "Your trial ended {endDate}." A shop told *six months*
  reads that in three weeks. Keeping the term out of the first email buys time on this; it does not
  fix it, and the first shop to accept will hit it.
- **"Six months" has no clock anywhere in the app.** No column, no timer, no reminder. Decide
  whether it starts at signup, at import, or at the first real trip, write it down, and use the
  same words every time
  ([FU-20260812](../follow-ups/FU-20260812-six-month-term-has-no-product-clock.md)).
- **The C-card and waiver wording is safety-adjacent copy** and carries the same
  `dive-domain-expert` bar as any other surface. Reviewed 2026-08-12; re-run that review if the
  Saturday or water paragraph is rewritten.
