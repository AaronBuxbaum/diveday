# Case-study interview template

Question list for the founder's interview with a design-partner or founding shop, once they've run
real dive days on DiveDay and agreed (per their pilot agreement's case-study/quote rights) to be
interviewed. Feeds the Phase 1 exit criterion ("a written case study draft with the shop's
numbers": [rollout.md](../rollout.md#phase-1--design-partners-septoct-2026)) and the Phase 2
founding-shop references work ([commercial-and-industry.md](commercial-and-industry.md#founding-shop-references-phase-2-day-30-check-ins)).
Governed by the [claims policy](../marketing.md) via the `commercial-outreach` skill.

**Hard rule: this is a template, not a draft case study.** Every number and quote below comes from
the shop's own mouth in the actual interview — never estimated, never extrapolated from the
metrics dashboard on their behalf, never written before the conversation happens. A case study
with placeholder or assumed numbers is a claims-policy violation the moment it's published; write
nothing here that looks like a filled example.

## Before the interview

- Confirm the pilot agreement's case-study/quote rights actually cover what's being asked (a named
  quote vs. a fully attributed case study are different asks — check which was agreed).
- Pull the shop's own [metrics](../rollout.md#metrics--the-scoreboard) as *prompts* for the
  conversation, not as the answer — ask the shop to confirm or correct every number before it's
  used.
- Bring the [design-partner one-pager](design-partner-one-pager.md) so the "before" state is fresh
  in both people's minds.

## Questions

### Before DiveDay

1. What were you using before (a competitor, spreadsheets, paper, a mix)? What made you look for
   something else?
2. Walk me through what a trip morning looked like before — who did what, where the friction was.
3. If you migrated from another system: what worried you about switching? What made the decision?

### Running it

4. What's changed about how the front desk, the boat, or a specific role's day actually runs?
5. Any specific morning, trip, or moment where DiveDay clearly helped (or clearly didn't)? Ask for
   the concrete story, not just a rating.
6. What number would you point to — time saved, a specific recurring problem that stopped, a
   diver's reaction? Ask the shop to state it themselves; don't suggest a figure.
7. Anything that broke, confused staff, or you had to work around? (This triages into the roadmap
   punch list regardless of whether it makes the case study — see
   [commercial-and-industry.md](commercial-and-industry.md#where-outcomes-land).)

### The quote

8. Ask directly for a sentence they'd be comfortable seeing published, in their own words. Read it
   back. Confirm exact wording and attribution (name, shop, role) they're comfortable with.
9. Confirm: can this be used on the website, in outreach to other shops, both? Any restriction
   (no logo, first-name only, etc.)?

## After the interview

- Write the case study using only what was said in the room — no smoothing a number, no combining
  two different remarks into one quote.
- Send the drafted quote/case study back to the shop for sign-off before it's used anywhere
  (matches `marketing.md`: "when real customers exist, their words go through the product owner
  first" — the shop's sign-off comes first, the product owner's second).
- Record the outcome in the [human-decisions.md](../human-decisions.md) verification queue (V-04)
  and, once published, add it as a real claim in [marketing.md](../marketing.md) rather than
  letting it live only in this interview record.
